## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This memorandum summarizes the material red flags and risks identified during comprehensive review of the Project Ridgeline data room documents in connection with the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES" or the "Company") by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP.

**Key Findings:**
- **3 CRITICAL red flags** requiring immediate resolution before closing
- **8 HIGH-severity risks** with material financial or operational impact
- **8 MEDIUM-severity risks** requiring active management and mitigation
- **1 LOW-severity risk** with minimal impact

**Critical Issues Requiring Immediate Action:**
1. **DOE Contract Revenue Cliff** — $12.1M+ revenue at risk in FY2025 if IDIQ ceiling not increased
2. **Debt Prepayment Obligation** — $39.2M+ cash requirement at closing plus refinancing risk
3. **Lease Assignment Unresolved** — Salt Lake City facility (12.7% of revenue) lacks landlord consent to assignment

## CRITICAL RED FLAGS

### RF-001: DOE Contract Revenue Concentration & Ceiling Exhaustion

**Issue:** The U.S. Department of Energy contract (DE-EM-0005523) represents 19.2% of trailing twelve-month revenue ($24.4M of $127.3M total). The IDIQ contract has a $45M ceiling, of which 72.7% ($32.7M) has been utilized through task orders, leaving only $12.3M remaining ceiling capacity.

**Risk:** At the current run rate of approximately $24.4M annually, the remaining $12.3M ceiling will be exhausted by April-May 2025, leaving approximately 16 months of the contract period (through August 31, 2026) unfunded. This creates a potential revenue cliff of $12.1M+ in FY2025, representing 9.5% of current total revenue.

**Financial Impact:** 
- Potential FY2025 revenue decline from $127.3M to approximately $115M or lower
- EBITDA impact of $2.2M-$2.7M (assuming 18-20% EBITDA margin)
- Significant earnings headwind if ceiling not increased or new contract awarded

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Immediate engagement with DOE Contracting Officer to discuss ceiling modification or new contract opportunities
2. Develop contingency revenue plan to offset potential DOE revenue decline
3. Accelerate business development efforts in commercial segment
4. Obtain written confirmation from DOE regarding ceiling status and future contract opportunities

---

### RF-003: Debt Prepayment Obligation & Change-of-Control Provision

**Issue:** The Company maintains a senior secured credit facility with High Plains National Bank totaling $38.7M outstanding ($12.4M revolving facility + $26.3M term loan). The credit agreement contains a mandatory prepayment provision triggered by a change of control, defined as acquisition of 50% or more of the Company's equity.

**Risk:** The proposed 100% stock acquisition by Sycamore Capital will trigger the change-of-control provision, requiring full prepayment of all outstanding obligations within 5 business days of closing. Additionally, the term loan is subject to a prepayment premium of 1.0% ($263,000) if prepayment occurs between January 17, 2025 and January 16, 2026.

**Financial Impact:**
- Estimated total prepayment requirement: $39.2M-$39.3M (including accrued interest and prepayment premium)
- Refinancing risk if High Plains National Bank unwilling to waive change-of-control provision
- Potential for lender to demand higher interest rates or additional fees for refinancing
- Significant cash requirement at closing reduces net proceeds to sellers

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Negotiate debt payoff or refinancing with High Plains National Bank immediately
2. Obtain written commitment letter from replacement lender prior to signing SPA
3. Budget prepayment premium in transaction economics
4. Evaluate alternative financing structures (e.g., seller financing, earnout provisions)
5. Confirm no other debt instruments contain similar change-of-control provisions

### RF-007: Salt Lake City Lease Assignment — Unresolved Landlord Consent

**Issue:** The Salt Lake City transfer station facility (2100 West North Temple, Salt Lake City, UT 84116) is leased from Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC under a commercial lease dated April 1, 2019. The lease contains an explicit change-of-control clause requiring prior written landlord consent for any assignment or change in tenant ownership.

**Critical Problem:** Following RES's acquisition of Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC on March 15, 2022, an assignment request was submitted to the landlord on April 12, 2022. **As of December 2024 (2.5+ years later), the landlord has NOT provided written consent to the assignment.** The facility has been operated by RES personnel since the Pinnacle acquisition, and rent payments have been accepted by the landlord without objection. However, the absence of formal consent creates a material legal risk.

**Risk:** The proposed Sycamore acquisition will trigger an additional change-of-control event under the lease. The landlord could:
- Withhold consent to the Sycamore transaction
- Demand lease modification, rent increase, or additional security deposit
- Terminate the lease for breach of the assignment provision
- Assert that RES has been in material breach since April 2022

**Financial Impact:**
- The Salt Lake City facility generates approximately $16.2M in annual revenue (12.7% of total revenue)
- Disruption or loss of this facility would materially impact operations and profitability
- Potential relocation costs, operational downtime, and customer relationship damage
- Estimated impact: $12M-$16M annual revenue at risk

**Recommended Actions:**
1. **BEFORE CLOSING:** Obtain written landlord consent to the Pinnacle-to-RES assignment (retroactive to April 2022)
2. **BEFORE CLOSING:** Obtain written landlord consent to the Sycamore acquisition
3. Negotiate lease amendment if landlord demands modifications
4. Evaluate alternative facility options in Salt Lake City area as contingency
5. Obtain representation and warranty in SPA regarding lease compliance and landlord consent

## HIGH-SEVERITY RED FLAGS

### RF-002: Customer Concentration Risk

**Issue:** The Company's top 10 customers represent 67% of trailing twelve-month revenue, with the top 5 customers representing 50.4%. The two largest customers (DOE and Consolidated Mining & Minerals Corp.) together represent 30.6% of revenue.

**Risk:** Loss of either major customer would materially impact revenue and profitability. Consolidated Mining's master services agreement includes a 60-day termination for convenience clause, creating vulnerability to customer loss with minimal notice.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Accelerate new customer acquisition in commercial segment
- Evaluate long-term contract opportunities with existing customers
- Develop customer retention strategy and relationship management plan
- Assess customer concentration metrics against industry benchmarks

### RF-004: Environmental Liability — Grand Junction Facility Contamination

**Issue:** The Company's owned headquarters facility at 8750 Industrial Parkway, Grand Junction, Colorado (appraised value $6.8M) has documented soil and groundwater contamination resulting from historical industrial operations predating RES's 2009 acquisition.

**Findings from Phase II ESA (June 2019):**
- Petroleum hydrocarbon contamination (diesel-range organics) in western portion of site
- Chlorinated solvent contamination (TCE, PCE) in eastern portion of site
- Estimated remediation cost: $1.8M-$2.6M (2019 dollars)
- Vapor intrusion risk in occupied vehicle maintenance building
- Potential off-site groundwater migration

**Risk:** 
- No updated Phase II ESA has been commissioned since June 2019 (5+ years ago)
- Actual remediation costs may exceed the $3.2M accrual if conditions have worsened
- Vapor intrusion assessment has not been completed for occupied building
- Off-site monitoring wells have not been installed to confirm plume containment
- Potential third-party liability if contamination has migrated off-site

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Commission updated Phase II ESA prior to closing
2. Conduct vapor intrusion assessment of occupied building
3. Evaluate installation of off-site downgradient monitoring wells
4. Confirm adequacy of $3.2M environmental remediation accrual
5. Obtain environmental representation and warranty in SPA
6. Consider environmental indemnification insurance

### RF-005: Regulatory Enforcement — CDPHE Notice of Violation

**Issue:** The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) issued a Notice of Violation (Docket No. CDPHE-HW-2024-0093) on July 18, 2024, alleging improper storage of hazardous waste at the Grand Junction facility.

**Dispute:** The parties disagree on the duration of the alleged violation:
- **Company Position:** 12-day violation period, resulting in maximum penalty of $450,000 (12 days × $37,500/day statutory maximum)
- **CDPHE Position:** 31-day violation period, resulting in maximum penalty of $1,162,500 (31 days × $37,500/day)

**Risk:** 
- Unresolved regulatory matter creates contingent liability
- Penalty exposure ranges from $200,000-$1,162,500 depending on violation period determination
- Settlement negotiations are ongoing but not yet concluded
- Potential for CDPHE to impose additional compliance requirements or facility restrictions

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Obtain settlement agreement or final penalty determination prior to closing
2. Ensure adequate reserve for worst-case penalty scenario
3. Obtain regulatory compliance representation and warranty in SPA
4. Evaluate impact on future permit renewals and regulatory relationships

### RF-006: Pending Litigation — Ramirez Personal Injury Action

**Issue:** A personal injury action was filed on August 12, 2024, in U.S. District Court, District of Wyoming (Case No. 2:24-cv-00187) by Miguel Ramirez, a former RES field technician.

**Allegations:** Plaintiff alleges occupational exposure to volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and heavy metals without adequate personal protective equipment (PPE) during work at a remediation site in Sheridan, Wyoming from March 2023 through January 2024.

**Damages:** Plaintiff seeks $4.8M in damages, including medical expenses, lost wages, pain and suffering, and punitive damages.

**Risk:**
- Coverage determination is pending with Frontier Specialty Insurance Group
- If coverage is denied, potential uninsured liability of $500,000-$1,500,000
- Litigation costs and management distraction
- Precedent for similar claims from other former employees
- Punitive damages claim creates additional exposure

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Obtain coverage determination from insurer immediately
2. Evaluate settlement strategy and potential settlement range
3. Assess potential for similar claims from other former employees
4. Obtain litigation tail coverage post-closing
5. Obtain representation regarding occupational safety and health compliance in SPA

### RF-009: EBITDA Adjustment Discrepancy — Valuation Risk

**Issue:** There is a material discrepancy between management's Adjusted EBITDA and the bank's definition of Consolidated EBITDA under the credit agreement.

**Valuation Impact:**
- **Management Adjusted EBITDA:** $22.8M (per CIM and QofE data request)
- **Bank Consolidated EBITDA:** $18.4M (per credit agreement definition)
- **Adjustment Difference:** $5.4M (23.4% variance)

**Adjustments Not Qualifying Under Bank Definition:**
- Owner compensation normalization: $950,000
- One-time litigation settlement: $1,200,000
- ERP implementation costs: $650,000
- Pinnacle acquisition integration costs: $800,000
- Asbestos abatement service line wind-down: $1,800,000

**Valuation Variance:**
- LOI proposes 8.0x-9.0x EBITDA multiple
- Using $22.8M adjusted EBITDA: $182M-$205M enterprise value
- Using $18.4M bank EBITDA: $147M-$166M enterprise value
- **Potential valuation gap: $35M-$58M**

**Risk:** The Quality of Earnings analysis by Bridgewater Forsyth Group may challenge the adjustment categories or identify additional issues affecting valuation. Buyer may refuse to accept management's adjustment methodology.

**Recommended Actions:**
1. Prepare detailed support documentation for each adjustment category
2. Benchmark valuation against comparable transactions in environmental services sector
3. Develop conservative adjustment scenarios for negotiation
4. Negotiate EBITDA definition and adjustment methodology in SPA
5. Consider earnout provisions tied to achievement of adjusted EBITDA targets

## MEDIUM-SEVERITY RED FLAGS

### RF-008: Permit Transfer Status — Salt Lake City Facility

**Issue:** The Salt Lake City transfer station permit (UTH-0441) remains in the name of Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC. A transfer application to RES has been pending with the Utah Division of Waste Management since March 2022 (2.5+ years).

**Risk:** Operational risk if permit transfer is not completed. Regulatory compliance uncertainty. Potential enforcement action if operations are conducted under the wrong permit holder name.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Expedite permit transfer application with Utah Division of Waste Management
- Obtain written confirmation of transfer status
- Ensure buyer understands permit transfer timeline and risks

### RF-011: Fleet Age & Deferred Capital Expenditures

**Issue:** 44.7% of the Company's fleet (38 of 85 units) is at or past stated useful life. Average fleet age is 8.2 years compared to a 7-year useful life. Estimated replacement cost for past-life units is $9.8M.

**Risk:** Significant catch-up capital expenditures will be required post-closing. Management estimates normalized annual maintenance capex of $4.0M-$4.5M, but addressing the backlog of deferred replacements would require approximately $3.3M/year for 3 years above normalized levels.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Develop fleet replacement plan for post-closing period
- Budget $3.3M+ annual capex for fleet renewal
- Assess equipment condition and maintenance costs
- Consider sale-leaseback options for fleet financing

---

### RF-012: Asbestos Long-Tail Liability

**Issue:** The Company operated an asbestos abatement service line from FY2012 through FY2023 (11 years), performing work at approximately 145 sites across Colorado, Utah, Wyoming, and Montana. Two workers' compensation claims from former employees alleging occupational respiratory exposure remain open.

**Risk:** Potential long-tail asbestos claims from employees or third parties. The current pollution liability policy has a retroactive date of January 1, 2015, which excludes coverage for asbestos work performed prior to 2015 (i.e., work conducted during 2012-2014). No tail coverage has been procured.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Obtain extended reporting period (tail) for pollution liability policy
- Evaluate asbestos-specific tail coverage
- Assess adequacy of existing reserves for known claims
- Obtain representation regarding asbestos compliance and historical work practices

---

### RF-013: Threatened Wage & Hour Litigation

**Issue:** In October 2024, RES received a demand letter from counsel representing approximately 8 former field technicians who were terminated in connection with the wind-down of the asbestos abatement service line in 2023. The demand alleges unpaid overtime compensation under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and Colorado Overtime and Minimum Pay Standards Order (COMPS Order).

**Claimed Exposure:** $165,000 in unpaid overtime wages plus $165,000 in liquidated damages under FLSA's double-damages provision, totaling $330,000.

**Risk:** Potential $100,000-$200,000 exposure if claim is filed and results in adverse outcome. Precedent for similar claims from other former employees. Management believes the motor carrier exemption may apply to some claimants, but this requires detailed analysis.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Obtain employment counsel analysis of wage classification
- Prepare defense strategy
- Evaluate settlement range
- Obtain representation regarding wage and hour compliance in SPA

### RF-014: Union Organizing Activity

**Issue:** An NLRB election petition was filed on November 4, 2024, by International Brotherhood of Teamsters Local 455 covering 62 field technicians at the Grand Junction and Casper facilities. The election date has not yet been scheduled.

**Risk:** Potential unionization of 12.8% of the workforce. Increased labor costs, reduced operational flexibility, and potential work stoppages. Buyer may face labor relations challenges post-closing.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Monitor NLRB election process
- Assess potential impact on labor costs and operations
- Evaluate buyer's labor relations strategy
- Obtain representation regarding labor matters in SPA

### RF-016: Revenue Growth Deceleration & Margin Compression

**Issue:** FY2024 revenue growth of 11.1% year-over-year is the lowest in the four-year period (FY2020-FY2024 CAGR: 16.8%). Additionally, FY2024 reported EBITDA declined 27% year-over-year ($26.0M in FY2023 vs. $18.4M in FY2024). Adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 18.7% from 24.5% in FY2023.

**Risk:** Slowing organic growth trajectory. Margin compression suggests operational challenges or one-time costs. May indicate market saturation or competitive pressure.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Conduct detailed analysis of FY2024 margin compression drivers
- Evaluate sustainability of historical growth rate
- Assess competitive positioning
- Develop post-closing growth strategy

### RF-018: Pinnacle Integration Risks

**Issue:** Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC was acquired on March 15, 2022, for $14.5M. While management describes the integration as "substantially complete," the entity has not been formally dissolved. The permit transfer for the Salt Lake City facility remains pending (2+ years post-acquisition), and the lease assignment has not been consented to by the landlord.

**Risk:** Incomplete integration creates operational and legal uncertainty. Unresolved permit and lease issues could disrupt operations. Buyer inherits integration risks.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Complete Pinnacle entity dissolution or formal wind-down
- Obtain permit transfer confirmation
- Resolve lease assignment with landlord
- Conduct post-acquisition integration review

### RF-019: Insurance Coverage Gaps

**Issue:** The Company's Commercial General Liability (CGL) policy contains a blanket asbestos exclusion. The Pollution Legal Liability (PLL) policy excludes coverage for gradual contamination at owned facilities (the Grand Junction site has documented gradual contamination). No tail coverage has been procured for asbestos-related work predating January 1, 2015.

**Risk:** Potential uninsured liability for asbestos claims and environmental contamination at the Grand Junction facility. Buyer may face uninsured losses.

**Recommended Actions:**
- Obtain extended reporting period (tail) for pollution liability
- Evaluate asbestos-specific tail coverage
- Assess adequacy of environmental remediation accrual
- Obtain insurance representation in SPA
