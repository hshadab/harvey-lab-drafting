## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This red flag analysis identifies 20 material issues across financial, operational, legal, regulatory, and environmental categories that require resolution or mitigation prior to closing of the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES") by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP. The analysis is based on comprehensive review of data room documents including financial statements, contracts, permits, litigation files, and regulatory correspondence.

### KEY FINDINGS

**FINDING 1 – CRITICAL REVENUE CONCENTRATION RISK:** The U.S. Department of Energy IDIQ contract represents 19.2% of trailing twelve-month revenue ($24.4M). The contract ceiling of $45M is 72.7% utilized with only $12.3M remaining. At current run rate, the ceiling will be exhausted by April-May 2025, leaving 16 months of unfunded contract period. Potential revenue decline of $12.1M+ in FY2025 (9.5% of total revenue) represents the single largest earnings headwind.

**FINDING 2 – MATERIAL DEBT REFINANCING OBLIGATION:** The senior secured credit facility with High Plains National Bank ($38.7M outstanding) contains a mandatory prepayment provision triggered by change of control. Upon closing, the entire outstanding balance plus accrued interest and a 1.0% prepayment premium ($263K) must be repaid within 5 business days. Total cash requirement at closing: approximately $39.2M-$39.3M. Significant refinancing risk if current lender unwilling to waive change-of-control provision.

**FINDING 3 – CRITICAL LEASE ASSIGNMENT CONSENT GAP:** The Salt Lake City transfer station lease (Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC) was originally executed by Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC. Assignment request to RES submitted April 12, 2022 but NOT YET CONSENTED TO by landlord (nearly 3 years later). The lease contains explicit change-of-control clause requiring landlord consent for the proposed Sycamore acquisition. Facility generates ~$16.2M annual revenue (12.7% of total). Landlord could withhold consent or demand unfavorable lease modifications.

**FINDING 4 – FOUNDER RETENTION & SEVERANCE RISK:** Derek and Jason Calloway (co-founders, 65% combined ownership) have single-trigger change-of-control severance provisions totaling $2.625M payable at closing regardless of post-closing employment status. This creates perverse incentive for founder departure immediately after receiving severance. Derek Calloway's departure would trigger Event of Default under credit facility. Loss of founder-led management would disrupt operations and impair buyer's integration strategy.

**FINDING 5 – MATERIAL ENVIRONMENTAL LIABILITY:** Grand Junction facility (owned, $6.8M appraised value) has documented soil and groundwater contamination. Phase II ESA (June 2019) estimated remediation cost of $1.8M-$2.6M. No updated ESA since 2019. Accrual of $3.2M on balance sheet may be inadequate if conditions have worsened. Vapor intrusion risk in occupied building. Potential off-site migration liability if plume extends beyond property boundary.

### AGGREGATE FINANCIAL EXPOSURE

Estimated $50M-$65M in contingent liabilities, debt repayment obligations, and capex requirements, representing 27-35% of the indicative enterprise value range ($182M-$205M).

## CRITICAL ISSUES REQUIRING IMMEDIATE ATTENTION

### 1. DOE CONTRACT CEILING EXHAUSTION (RF-001)

The U.S. Department of Energy IDIQ contract (DE-EM-0005523) represents 19.2% of trailing twelve-month revenue ($24.4M). The contract has a $45M ceiling, of which $32.7M has been utilized (72.7%), leaving only $12.3M remaining. At the current run rate of approximately $24.4M annually, the remaining ceiling will be exhausted by April-May 2025, leaving approximately 16 months of unfunded contract period through the August 31, 2026 contract expiration.

**IMPACT:** Potential revenue decline of $12.1M+ in FY2025 (9.5% of total revenue), representing a material earnings headwind. This is the single largest revenue concentration risk in the portfolio.

**REQUIRED ACTION:**
- Immediate engagement with DOE Contracting Officer to discuss ceiling modification or new contract opportunities
- Develop contingency revenue plan for FY2025 assuming ceiling exhaustion
- Evaluate alternative government contract opportunities
- Assess impact on valuation and purchase price

### 2. DEBT CHANGE-OF-CONTROL PREPAYMENT OBLIGATION (RF-003)

The senior secured credit facility with High Plains National Bank ($38.7M outstanding) contains a mandatory prepayment provision triggered by change of control. Upon closing, the entire outstanding balance ($38.7M) plus accrued interest and a prepayment premium must be repaid within 5 business days.

**PREPAYMENT PREMIUM:** If closing occurs between January 17, 2025 and January 16, 2026, the prepayment premium is 1.0% of the term loan principal ($26.3M), equaling approximately $263,000. If closing occurs after January 16, 2026, no premium applies.

**TOTAL CASH REQUIREMENT AT CLOSING:** Approximately $39.2M-$39.3M (principal + interest + premium).

**IMPACT:** Significant refinancing risk. If High Plains National Bank is unwilling to waive the change-of-control provision, buyer must arrange alternative financing or seller must arrange payoff from transaction proceeds.

**REQUIRED ACTION:**
- Immediately contact High Plains National Bank to discuss refinancing options
- Obtain commitment letter from replacement lender (if refinancing)
- Negotiate payoff letter with current lender
- Budget prepayment premium in transaction economics
- Confirm no other debt instruments contain similar change-of-control provisions

### 3. SALT LAKE CITY LEASE ASSIGNMENT CONSENT GAP (RF-007)

The Salt Lake City transfer station lease (Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC, 2100 West North Temple) was originally executed by Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC. Following RES's acquisition of Pinnacle in March 2022, an assignment request was submitted to the landlord on April 12, 2022. As of December 2024 (nearly 3 years later), the landlord has NOT PROVIDED WRITTEN CONSENT to the assignment.

**LEASE TERMS:** The lease contains an explicit change-of-control clause requiring landlord consent for any assignment or change of control of the tenant. The proposed Sycamore acquisition would constitute an additional change-of-control event, potentially requiring separate landlord consent.

**REVENUE AT RISK:** The Salt Lake City facility serves customers generating approximately $16.2M in annual revenue (12.7% of total company revenue), including Wasatch Front Petroleum ($6.4M), Silver Creek Mining ($5.8M), Utah DEQ ($4.6M), and others.

**IMPACT:** Landlord could withhold consent, demand lease modification, or impose unfavorable terms (e.g., rent increase, shorter renewal period). Facility disruption would materially impact operations and revenue.

**REQUIRED ACTION:**
- Immediately contact Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC to obtain written consent to Pinnacle-to-RES assignment (cure pre-existing gap)
- Obtain separate written consent to Sycamore acquisition
- Negotiate lease amendment if landlord demands modifications
- Evaluate alternative facility options in Salt Lake City area
- Obtain landlord consent as a closing condition in SPA

### 4. FOUNDER RETENTION & CHANGE-OF-CONTROL SEVERANCE (RF-010, RF-020)

Derek Calloway (CEO, 32.5% owner) and Jason Calloway (COO, 32.5% owner) have employment agreements containing single-trigger change-of-control severance provisions. Upon closing of the proposed transaction, each is entitled to a lump-sum severance payment of 18 months of base salary ($875,000 × 1.5 = $1,312,500 each), totaling $2,625,000 combined.

**CRITICAL ISSUE:** The severance is payable at closing regardless of whether the executives' employment continues, is modified, or is terminated post-closing. This creates a perverse incentive for the founders to depart immediately after receiving severance payments.

**ADDITIONAL RISK:** Derek Calloway is designated as the required CEO under the credit facility. His departure would trigger an Event of Default under the High Plains National Bank credit agreement, potentially accelerating the debt repayment obligation.

**IMPACT:** Loss of founder-led management team would disrupt operations, damage customer relationships, and impair buyer's ability to execute post-closing integration and growth strategy.

**REQUIRED ACTION:**
- Negotiate post-closing employment/retention agreements with Calloway brothers
- Consider earnout provisions tied to revenue/EBITDA targets over 2-3 year period
- Evaluate non-compete enforceability and post-severance restrictions
- Develop management succession plan for key operational roles
- Obtain representations regarding management continuity in SPA

## HIGH SEVERITY ISSUES

The following eight issues present material financial, operational, or legal risks that require resolution or mitigation prior to closing:

**RF-002: CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION** – Top 10 customers represent 67% of revenue; top 5 represent 50.4%. Consolidated Mining & Minerals Corp. (11.4% of revenue) has 60-day termination for convenience clause. Loss of major customer would materially impact revenue and profitability.

**RF-004: ENVIRONMENTAL LIABILITY** – Grand Junction facility (owned, $6.8M appraised value) has documented soil and groundwater contamination. Phase II ESA (June 2019) estimated remediation cost of $1.8M-$2.6M. No updated ESA since 2019. Accrual of $3.2M on balance sheet may be inadequate if conditions have worsened. Vapor intrusion risk in occupied building.

**RF-005: REGULATORY ENFORCEMENT** – CDPHE Notice of Violation (Docket CDPHE-HW-2024-0093) issued July 18, 2024 for improper hazardous waste storage. Company asserts 12-day violation period; CDPHE asserts 31-day period. Potential penalty range: $200K-$450K (company position) to $1.16M (CDPHE position). Settlement negotiations ongoing.

**RF-006: LITIGATION RISK** – Ramirez v. RES personal injury action (U.S. District Court, D. Wyoming, Case 2:24-cv-00187) filed August 12, 2024. Former field technician alleges occupational exposure to VOCs/heavy metals without adequate PPE. Damages sought: $4.8M. Coverage determination pending with Frontier Specialty Insurance Group. Potential uninsured liability of $500K-$1.5M.

**RF-009: EBITDA ADJUSTMENT DISPUTES** – Management Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M includes $5.4M in adjustments that do NOT qualify under bank definition of Consolidated EBITDA ($18.4M). Valuation gap of $35M-$58M depending on which EBITDA metric is used. Buyer may challenge adjustment categories.

**RF-011: FLEET AGE & CAPEX** – 44.7% of fleet (38 of 85 units) at or past stated useful life. Average fleet age 8.2 years vs. 7-year useful life. Estimated replacement cost for past-life units: $9.8M. Significant catch-up capex required post-closing ($3.3M/year for 3 years).

**RF-012: ASBESTOS TAIL LIABILITY** – Company operated asbestos abatement service line FY2012-FY2023. Two open workers' compensation claims from former employees alleging occupational respiratory exposure. Pollution liability policy retroactive date: January 1, 2015 (excludes pre-2015 work). Unquantifiable but potentially material exposure.

## CONCLUSION

The proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. presents a compelling platform opportunity in a fragmented, high-barrier-to-entry environmental services market. However, the transaction is subject to material risks across financial, operational, legal, regulatory, and environmental categories that require careful management and resolution prior to closing.

The four critical issues identified above—DOE contract ceiling exhaustion, debt change-of-control prepayment, Salt Lake City lease assignment consent, and founder retention—must be resolved as preconditions to closing. The eight high-severity issues require mitigation through updated due diligence, regulatory engagement, and transaction documentation.

Aggregate financial exposure across all identified issues is estimated at $50M-$65M, representing 27-35% of the indicative enterprise value range. This exposure should be factored into valuation, purchase price adjustments, and post-closing integration planning.

With appropriate due diligence, negotiation, and transaction structuring, these risks can be effectively managed and mitigated. However, failure to address the critical issues prior to closing would expose the buyer to material operational, financial, and legal risks post-acquisition.
