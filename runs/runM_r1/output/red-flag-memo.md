## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This memorandum summarizes material red flags and diligence concerns identified through a comprehensive review of the Project Ridgeline data room, including the Confidential Information Memorandum, Quality of Earnings data package, Litigation and Regulatory Summary, Real Property and Lease Schedule, Phase II Environmental Site Assessment, Credit Agreement Summary, Customer Contract Summary, Employment Agreements Summary, Environmental Permit Schedule, Insurance Program Summary, and Employee Benefits Summary.

**Thirteen (13) distinct red flags** have been identified, spanning six risk categories: (1) Financial / Quality of Earnings, (2) Environmental & Regulatory, (3) Commercial / Customer Concentration, (4) Legal & Litigation, (5) Structural / Transactional, and (6) Operational. Each flag is rated by **Severity** (Critical / High / Medium) and **Likelihood of Impact** (High / Medium / Low).

The most critical issues are: (a) a material EBITDA discrepancy between the CIM and the QofE data package that calls into question the reliability of the valuation anchor; (b) the DOE IDIQ contract ceiling exhaustion, which threatens approximately $12.1 million of FY2025 revenue; (c) unresolved environmental contamination at the owned Grand Junction facility with a stale 2019 cost estimate; (d) the Salt Lake City lease and permit double-jeopardy (unobtained landlord consent and pending permit transfer); and (e) $3.775 million in change-of-control severance obligations whose allocation between buyer and seller has not been addressed in the LOI.

Buyer should not proceed to signing without resolving or appropriately pricing each Critical and High severity item.

## II. RED FLAG DETAIL

### RED FLAG 1 — EBITDA Discrepancy: CIM vs. QofE Data Package

**Category:** Financial / Quality of Earnings
**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** The CIM presents FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA of **$22.8 million**. The QofE data package (EBITDA Bridge & Adjustments tab) calculates Management Adjusted EBITDA of **$23.8 million** — a **$1.0 million discrepancy** that management has acknowledged but not yet reconciled. The specific adjustment category causing the difference has not been identified. The LOI enterprise value range of $182–$205 million is anchored to the $22.8 million figure; at 8.0x–9.0x, a $1.0 million EBITDA swing equates to an $8–$9 million valuation difference.

**Additional Concern:** The QofE data package also reveals that the bank's definition of Consolidated EBITDA (per the High Plains National Bank credit agreement) produces only **$18.4 million** — approximately $4.4 million below management's adjusted figure — because the credit agreement expressly disallows add-backs for owner compensation normalization, ERP costs, acquisition integration costs, asbestos wind-down costs, and litigation settlements. The $4.4 million in adjustments being added back to reach the $22.8 million figure are all excluded under the bank covenant definition.

**Required Action:** Demand a fully reconciled EBITDA bridge before signing. Engage Bridgewater Forsyth Group to independently validate each add-back. Scrutinize the $950K owner compensation normalization (founders are being paid $875K each; market rate is asserted at $400K each — buyer must confirm replacement cost and whether founders will accept reduced compensation post-close). Confirm that the $1.2 million litigation settlement add-back is a receipt (income), not a cost, and that it is properly characterized as non-recurring.

### RED FLAG 2 — DOE IDIQ Contract Ceiling Exhaustion: ~$12.1M FY2025 Revenue Headwind

**Category:** Commercial / Customer Concentration
**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** The U.S. Department of Energy is the Company's largest customer at **$24.4 million (19.2% of TTM revenue)**. Revenue is generated under IDIQ Contract No. DE-EM-0005523 (5-year term, $45.0 million ceiling, expiring August 31, 2026). As of the data room cutoff, **$32.7 million of the $45.0 million ceiling has been consumed** (72.7% utilization) with only **61.7% of the contract period elapsed**. At the current run rate of approximately $2.03 million per month, the remaining $12.3 million ceiling will be **exhausted by approximately April–May 2025** — coinciding with the target closing date. The contract does not expire until August 2026, leaving approximately 16 months of unfunded contract period.

**Implication:** Unless DOE issues a ceiling modification or a new contract is awarded, FY2025 DOE revenue could decline from $24.4 million to $12.3 million or less — a **$12.1 million revenue headwind representing approximately 9.5% of total revenue**. This is not disclosed in the CIM, which presents the DOE relationship as a "cornerstone" providing "substantial near-term revenue visibility."

**Additional Concern:** The contract is non-assignable without Contracting Officer consent per the Anti-Assignment Act (41 U.S.C. § 6305) and FAR 42.12. Contract Section H, Clause H-12 requires written notification to the Contracting Officer within 30 days of any change in ownership. Failure to notify may be grounds for termination for default.

**Required Action:** Obtain written confirmation from DOE Contracting Officer regarding ceiling modification prospects and novation requirements before signing. Model FY2025 and FY2026 revenue scenarios assuming no ceiling increase. Adjust enterprise value downward if ceiling exhaustion is confirmed. Require a specific rep and warranty regarding DOE contract status and ceiling availability.

### RED FLAG 3 — Grand Junction Environmental Contamination: Stale ESA, Unquantified Upside Risk

**Category:** Environmental & Regulatory
**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** The Company's owned Grand Junction headquarters (8750 Industrial Parkway) has documented soil and groundwater contamination from pre-acquisition industrial operations, confirmed by a Phase II ESA completed in **June 2019** — over five years ago. The ESA identified:

- **Petroleum hydrocarbon contamination** (TPH-diesel up to 4,200 mg/kg; benzene in groundwater at 12 µg/L, exceeding Colorado standards) in the former UST area.
- **Chlorinated solvent contamination** (PCE up to 18 mg/kg in soil; PCE at 22 µg/L and TCE at 8.7 µg/L in groundwater, both exceeding Colorado standards) in the former degreasing area.
- **Potential vapor intrusion** into the occupied 28,000 sq. ft. vehicle maintenance building (PCE at 450 µg/m³ and TCE at 120 µg/m³ in soil gas, both exceeding EPA RSLs). No vapor intrusion assessment has been conducted.
- **Unconfirmed plume boundaries**: the 2019 ESA explicitly recommended off-site sentinel monitoring wells to confirm the plume has not migrated toward the Colorado River drainage system. These wells have not been installed.

The 2019 cost estimate of **$1.8–$2.6 million** is in 2019 dollars, has not been updated, and expressly excludes: vapor intrusion assessment/mitigation, off-site remediation (if plume has migrated), and DNAPL migration costs. The Company has accrued $2.2 million for Grand Junction (within a $3.2 million total accrual), which may be materially insufficient.

**The Pollution Legal Liability policy explicitly excludes gradual contamination at owned facilities** — the exact characterization applied to this contamination in the ESA. There is no insurance backstop for this liability.

**Required Action:** Commission an updated Phase II ESA and vapor intrusion assessment immediately, at seller's expense, as a condition to signing. Install off-site sentinel monitoring wells. Obtain an independent remediation cost estimate in current dollars. Negotiate a specific environmental indemnity with a cap above the general indemnity cap and a 6-year survival period (already contemplated in the LOI). Consider a separate environmental escrow.

### RED FLAG 4 — Salt Lake City Lease: Unobtained Landlord Consent (Dual Jeopardy)

**Category:** Structural / Transactional
**Severity:** CRITICAL
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** The Salt Lake City transfer station (2100 West North Temple) is the Company's second-largest facility by revenue contribution (~$16.2 million, or ~12.7% of total revenue). The lease was originally executed by **Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC** in 2019. Section 14 of the lease defines any change of control of the tenant as an assignment requiring landlord consent. RES requested consent on **April 12, 2022** following the Pinnacle acquisition. As of December 2024 — **over 2.5 years later** — the landlord (Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC) has **not provided written consent or acknowledgment**.

The proposed Sycamore acquisition would constitute a **second, independent change-of-control event** under the same clause, requiring a separate consent. The Company is therefore in a position of: (1) an unresolved pre-existing assignment default, and (2) a pending additional consent requirement for the proposed transaction. The landlord has accepted rent without objection, but this does not cure the default under Colorado/Utah law.

**Compounding Factor:** The same landlord (Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC) is listed as an additional insured on the CGL policy, suggesting an active relationship — yet has not responded to the consent request in over two years, which may indicate a deliberate posture.

**Required Action:** Obtain landlord consent as a hard closing condition. Engage directly with Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC before signing. Evaluate whether the landlord's silence constitutes a waiver under Utah law (unlikely). Assess termination risk: if the landlord terminates the lease, $16.2 million of annual revenue and the Utah hazardous waste permit (UTH-0441) are at risk. Consider a lease cure escrow or purchase price holdback tied to consent resolution.

### RED FLAG 5 — Salt Lake City Permit Transfer: Still in Pinnacle's Name After 2.5 Years

**Category:** Environmental & Regulatory
**Severity:** HIGH
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** Utah hazardous waste transfer station permit **UTH-0441** for the Salt Lake City facility remains in the name of **Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC** — not RES — as of December 2024, more than 2.5 years after the Pinnacle acquisition. The transfer application was initiated but has not been completed. Under Utah Administrative Code R315-3, permit modifications reflecting changes in ownership require Division of Waste Management approval before becoming effective.

**Risk:** RES is operating the facility under a permit issued to a dormant subsidiary. If the Utah Division of Waste Management determines that operations are being conducted without a properly transferred permit, it could issue a notice of violation, require cessation of operations, or deny the transfer application. The facility generates approximately $16.2 million in annual revenue and serves multiple top-20 customers.

**Compounding Factor:** The proposed Sycamore acquisition may trigger a new permit modification requirement, further complicating the transfer process.

**Required Action:** Obtain written confirmation from the Utah Division of Waste Management regarding the status of the transfer application and the Division's position on the current operating arrangement. Make permit transfer completion a closing condition. Assess whether the proposed transaction requires a separate permit modification filing.

### RED FLAG 6 — CDPHE Enforcement Action: Unaccrued Penalty Exposure Up to $1.16 Million

**Category:** Environmental & Regulatory
**Severity:** HIGH
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** CDPHE issued a Notice of Violation (Docket No. CDPHE-HW-2024-0093) on July 18, 2024, alleging improper storage of approximately 14,000 gallons of F001/F002 listed hazardous waste at the Grand Junction facility in an unauthorized staging area beyond the 90-day accumulation limit. The parties dispute the violation period:

- **Company position:** 12-day violation → maximum penalty of **$450,000** (12 × $37,500/day statutory maximum).
- **CDPHE position:** 31-day violation → maximum penalty of **$1,162,500** (31 × $37,500/day).

**No accrual has been recorded** on the Company's balance sheet as of September 30, 2024. Management's estimated settlement range is $200,000–$450,000, but this is based on the Company's own violation period characterization, which CDPHE disputes. The matter is in administrative negotiation; a settlement conference is expected in Q1 2025.

**Additional Concern:** The CDPHE enforcement action is an Event of Default under the credit agreement if it results in a governmental order requiring remediation in excess of $1.0 million. While the current matter involves penalties rather than remediation, the interaction with the credit agreement should be evaluated.

**Required Action:** Require the Company to resolve the CDPHE matter before closing, or establish a specific indemnity escrow for the full CDPHE exposure ($1.16 million). Obtain CDPHE's current negotiating position in writing. Confirm that the enforcement action does not jeopardize the RCRA Part B permit (COD-098-2761), which expires December 31, 2026 and is the Company's most critical operating permit.

### RED FLAG 7 — Wyoming Permit Expired: Operating Under Timely Renewal Provisions

**Category:** Environmental & Regulatory
**Severity:** HIGH
**Likelihood of Impact:** Medium

**Finding:** Wyoming hazardous waste permit **WY-HW-2019-033** for the Casper facility **expired November 30, 2024**. A renewal application was filed October 15, 2024 (46 days before expiration). The Company is currently operating under Wyoming's "timely renewal" provisions, which allow continued operations while a renewal application is pending. The renewal is expected in Q1 2025.

**Risk:** While timely renewal provisions are standard, the permit is technically expired. Any compliance issue at the Casper facility during the renewal period could complicate or jeopardize the renewal. The Casper facility employs 68 people and serves approximately $30.2 million of Wyoming-based revenue. Under the credit agreement, revocation or non-renewal of a material permit is an Event of Default.

**Required Action:** Confirm Wyoming DEQ's written acknowledgment of the timely renewal filing. Obtain a status update from Wyoming DEQ on the renewal timeline. Make permit renewal a closing condition or obtain a specific representation regarding permit status.

### RED FLAG 8 — Change-of-Control Severance: $3.775 Million Unallocated in LOI

**Category:** Structural / Transactional
**Severity:** HIGH
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** All five executive employment agreements contain change-of-control severance provisions triggered by the proposed 100% stock acquisition. Total aggregate severance is **$3,775,000**:

| Executive | Amount | Trigger Type |
|---|---|---|
| Derek Calloway (CEO) | $1,312,500 | **Single trigger** — payable at closing |
| Jason Calloway (COO) | $1,312,500 | **Single trigger** — payable at closing |
| Patricia Engel (CFO) | $425,000 | Double trigger |
| Kurt Dillon (VP Ops) | $375,000 | Double trigger |
| Sandra Oakes (VP BD) | $350,000 | Double trigger |
| **Total** | **$3,775,000** | |

The **single-trigger** Calloway payments ($2,625,000 combined) are payable within 30 days of closing regardless of whether the founders continue in their roles. The LOI's definition of "Transaction Expenses" does not expressly address these severance obligations, and the LOI is silent on whether they are seller-borne or buyer-borne.

**Additional Concern:** The QofE data package notes the same issue and flags that classification has "not been addressed in the LOI or this data package." If treated as Transaction Expenses (seller-borne), they reduce the purchase price. If buyer-borne, they represent an additional $2.625–$3.775 million cash outlay at closing.

**Required Action:** Negotiate the treatment of CoC severance in the SPA before signing. Confirm that the Calloway founders will execute new employment agreements with market-rate compensation post-close (the $950K owner comp normalization EBITDA add-back assumes replacement at $400K each — but the founders' existing agreements entitle them to $875K each). Evaluate whether the single-trigger structure can be renegotiated to double-trigger in exchange for retention incentives.

### RED FLAG 9 — Asbestos Long-Tail Liability: Pre-2015 Coverage Gap

**Category:** Legal & Litigation
**Severity:** HIGH
**Likelihood of Impact:** Medium

**Finding:** RES operated an asbestos abatement service line from **2012 through September 30, 2023**, performing work at approximately 145 commercial and industrial sites across Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming. The current Pollution Legal Liability policy has a **retroactive date of January 1, 2015**, meaning work performed from **2012 through 2014** is not covered under the current policy. The CGL policy contains a **blanket asbestos exclusion** with no completed operations carve-out. No tail coverage or extended completed operations coverage for asbestos work has been procured.

**Risk:** Asbestos-related bodily injury claims can have latency periods of 10–40 years. Two workers' compensation claims from former asbestos abatement employees remain open (filed 2021 and 2022), alleging occupational respiratory exposure. While management rates future third-party asbestos claims as "Remote," the 2012–2014 coverage gap represents an uninsured tail exposure for which no reserve has been established.

**Required Action:** Obtain a complete claims history for the asbestos abatement service line. Quantify the 2012–2014 exposure (revenue of approximately $7.3 million over 3 years across ~40 sites). Require the sellers to procure a tail policy covering the 2012–2014 period as a closing condition, or negotiate a specific asbestos indemnity with a separate escrow. Confirm the status of the two open workers' compensation claims.

### RED FLAG 10 — Ramirez Personal Injury Lawsuit: Coverage Uncertainty

**Category:** Legal & Litigation
**Severity:** HIGH
**Likelihood of Impact:** Medium

**Finding:** *Ramirez v. Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc.* (D. Wyo., Case No. 2:24-cv-00187, filed August 12, 2024) is a personal injury action by a former field technician alleging inadequate PPE and air monitoring during VOC/heavy metal exposure at a Sheridan, Wyoming remediation site. Plaintiff seeks **$4.8 million** (including punitive damages). Management's estimated loss range is $500,000–$1,500,000.

**Critical Issue:** The insurer (Frontier Specialty Insurance Group) has issued a **reservation of rights letter** dated September 19, 2024. A coverage determination remains pending. If coverage is denied under both the CGL and pollution liability policies, the Company faces uninsured exposure of up to $1.5 million (management's estimate) or potentially higher. No accrual has been recorded.

**Required Action:** Obtain a copy of the reservation of rights letter and the insurer's coverage analysis. Assess the basis for the coverage dispute. Require a specific indemnity for the Ramirez matter. Confirm that the matter does not trigger the credit agreement's cross-default provision (judgment in excess of $1.0 million not covered by insurance).

### RED FLAG 11 — Fleet Condition: 44.7% of Assets Past Useful Life, $9.8M Replacement Backlog

**Category:** Operational
**Severity:** HIGH
**Likelihood of Impact:** High

**Finding:** The Fleet & Equipment Schedule reveals that **38 of 85 fleet and equipment assets (44.7%) are at or past their stated useful life**, with an average fleet age of **8.2 years** against a weighted average useful life of 7.0 years. Many are marked "Replacement Deferred" or "At End of Life — No Replacement Scheduled." The estimated replacement cost for past-useful-life units alone is **$9.8 million**.

Management's normalized maintenance capex estimate of $4.0–$4.5 million per year is inconsistent with the fleet data. Spreading the $9.8 million replacement backlog over 3 years implies approximately **$3.3 million per year in catch-up capex above the current run-rate**, suggesting true normalized maintenance capex is closer to $7.3–$7.8 million annually — nearly double management's estimate. This has a direct impact on free cash flow and, if capitalized at the transaction multiple, represents a **$26–$35 million valuation overstatement** embedded in the management capex assumption.

**Required Action:** Commission an independent fleet appraisal and condition assessment. Recast normalized maintenance capex using the fleet schedule data. Adjust the EBITDA-to-free-cash-flow bridge accordingly. Negotiate a purchase price reduction or escrow for deferred fleet replacement costs.

### RED FLAG 12 — Unionization Petition: NLRB Election Filed November 2024

**Category:** Operational
**Severity:** MEDIUM
**Likelihood of Impact:** Medium

**Finding:** A footnote in the Employee Benefits Summary discloses that the Company received notice of an **NLRB election petition filed November 4, 2024**, by the International Brotherhood of Teamsters Local 455, covering **62 field technicians at the Grand Junction and Casper facilities**. This disclosure is buried in a footnote and is not mentioned in the CIM, the Litigation and Regulatory Summary, or any other primary data room document.

**Risk:** If the union election succeeds, the Company would be required to bargain collectively with the Teamsters, potentially increasing labor costs, reducing operational flexibility, and affecting the Company's non-unionized status — which is presented as a competitive advantage in the CIM. The 62 affected employees represent approximately 13% of the total workforce and are concentrated at the two largest operational facilities.

**Required Action:** Obtain a full briefing on the NLRB petition, including the election timeline, the Company's response strategy, and any labor relations issues that may have precipitated the petition. Assess the financial impact of potential unionization on labor costs and operational flexibility. Require disclosure of any related employee relations issues or prior organizing activity.
