## I. Executive Summary

This memorandum summarizes material red flags identified across the Project Ridgeline data room in connection with the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES" or the "Company") by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP ("Buyer"). The analysis is based on a review of thirteen data room documents listed in Section II below.

**Seventeen distinct red flags have been identified**, spanning six risk categories: (1) Financial / Quality of Earnings, (2) Environmental & Regulatory, (3) Commercial / Customer Concentration, (4) Legal & Litigation, (5) Operational & Asset Condition, and (6) Structural / Transaction Mechanics. Each flag is rated by severity (Critical / High / Medium) and accompanied by a recommended action.

**The most critical issues are:**

- A **$1.0 million unexplained discrepancy** between the CIM's stated Adjusted EBITDA ($22.8M) and the QofE schedule ($23.8M), with no reconciliation provided — directly affecting the LOI enterprise value range of $182M–$205M.
- The **DOE IDIQ contract ceiling is 72.7% consumed** with only $12.3M remaining, implying a potential **$12.1M revenue headwind** in FY2025 — the year immediately following closing — against a contract that does not expire until August 2026.
- **Unresolved Salt Lake City lease assignment** (Pinnacle-to-RES, pending since April 2022) creates a pre-existing default risk at a facility generating ~$16.2M of annual revenue, compounded by a second change-of-control event triggered by the proposed acquisition.
- **No updated Phase II ESA since 2019** at the owned Grand Junction headquarters, where confirmed soil and groundwater contamination (PCE, TCE, petroleum hydrocarbons) carries a 2019-vintage cost estimate of $1.8M–$2.6M that is almost certainly stale, with vapor intrusion risk to an occupied building unassessed.
- **$3.775M in change-of-control severance** is triggered at closing (at least $2.625M on a single-trigger basis), with no allocation in the LOI's Transaction Expenses definition.

#### RF-01 | CRITICAL | Adjusted EBITDA Discrepancy — CIM vs. QofE Schedule ($1.0M Gap, Unreconciled)

**Source Documents:** CIM (§VI.B); QofE Data Request Response (EBITDA Bridge tab)

**Finding:** The CIM states FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA of **$22.8 million** (the basis for the LOI's 8.0x–9.0x multiple and $182M–$205M enterprise value range). The QofE EBITDA Bridge schedule, prepared by the Company's CFO for the Bridgewater Forsyth Group engagement, calculates Adjusted EBITDA of **$23.8 million** — a **$1.0 million difference**. The QofE schedule itself contains a reconciliation note acknowledging the discrepancy and stating that "the specific adjustment category causing the discrepancy has not been identified." No updated schedule has been provided.

**Risk:** At an 8.0x–9.0x multiple, a $1.0M EBITDA discrepancy implies a **$8M–$9M enterprise value swing**. If the true Adjusted EBITDA is $22.8M (as stated in the CIM), the QofE schedule overstates earnings. If it is $23.8M, the CIM understates earnings. Either way, the Company cannot reconcile its own earnings presentation — a fundamental quality-of-earnings concern. The discrepancy may reflect an error, a double-counted adjustment, or an undisclosed item.

**Recommended Action:** Demand a fully reconciled EBITDA bridge with line-item support before proceeding. Require the QofE firm (Bridgewater Forsyth Group) to independently verify and sign off on the final Adjusted EBITDA figure. Do not finalize enterprise value until resolved.

#### RF-02 | HIGH | Aggressive EBITDA Adjustments — Recurring Items Characterized as Non-Recurring

**Source Documents:** QofE Data Request Response (EBITDA Bridge tab); CIM (§VI.B)

**Finding:** The Company's $4.4M–$5.4M in EBITDA adjustments include several items that warrant scrutiny as to their non-recurring characterization:

- **Pinnacle Acquisition Integration Costs ($800K in FY2024):** The Pinnacle acquisition closed in March 2022 — over two years before the TTM period. Integration costs of $800K in FY2024 (and $620K in FY2023) suggest integration is not "substantially complete" as represented. These costs have been added back in each of FY2022, FY2023, and FY2024, totaling $2.66M over three years.
- **Asbestos Abatement Wind-Down ($1.8M in FY2024):** The service line was discontinued effective September 30, 2023. Wind-down costs of $1.8M in FY2024 are added back as non-recurring; however, two workers' compensation claims from former asbestos employees remain open, and the litigation summary notes potential long-tail exposure. Future costs may arise.
- **ERP Implementation ($650K in FY2024):** The QofE schedule notes that "Phase 2 [is] expected to be completed Q2 FY2025," meaning additional implementation costs are likely in the next fiscal year and should not be treated as fully non-recurring.
- **Owner Compensation Normalization ($950K):** The adjustment normalizes CEO and COO compensation from $875K each to $400K each. However, the employment agreements confirm both founders receive single-trigger change-of-control severance of $1.3125M each at closing — meaning the "normalized" replacement cost assumption is immediately invalidated post-closing.

**Risk:** If even $1.5M–$2.0M of the adjustments are deemed recurring by the QofE firm, Adjusted EBITDA falls to $20.8M–$21.3M, implying an enterprise value of $166M–$192M at the same multiples — a material reduction from the LOI range.

**Recommended Action:** Require the QofE firm to independently assess the non-recurring characterization of each adjustment. Negotiate a purchase price adjustment mechanism tied to the QofE firm's final Adjusted EBITDA conclusion.

#### RF-03 | HIGH | Reported EBITDA Declined 27% Year-over-Year (FY2023 to FY2024)

**Source Documents:** QofE Data Request Response (Historical P&L tab)

**Finding:** The Historical P&L tab shows Reported EBITDA (before adjustments) of **$25.2M in FY2023** declining to **$18.4M in FY2024** — a **27.0% year-over-year decline** in reported earnings. Revenue growth slowed from 17.2% (FY2023) to 11.1% (FY2024). The CIM presents only Adjusted EBITDA and does not disclose the magnitude of the reported EBITDA decline.

**Risk:** The 27% decline in reported EBITDA is masked by $5.4M in adjustments. The underlying business generated materially less earnings in FY2024 than FY2023 on a reported basis. This trend, combined with revenue growth deceleration, raises questions about whether the business is experiencing margin compression or cost escalation that is being obscured by the adjustment schedule.

**Recommended Action:** Obtain a detailed bridge from FY2023 to FY2024 reported EBITDA explaining the $6.8M decline. Assess whether any portion reflects structural margin deterioration rather than truly non-recurring items.

#### RF-04 | HIGH | Bank EBITDA Definition Produces $18.4M vs. Management's $22.8M–$23.8M — Covenant Headroom Narrower Than Presented

**Source Documents:** Credit Agreement Summary (§4); QofE Data Request Response (EBITDA Bridge tab)

**Finding:** The Credit Agreement with High Plains National Bank defines "Consolidated EBITDA" to exclude all of the management adjustments (owner comp normalization, integration costs, wind-down costs, ERP costs, and litigation settlement), permitting only up to $500K of non-recurring charges. The bank's Consolidated EBITDA for the TTM period is approximately **$18.4M** — the reported figure. Total leverage under the bank definition is **2.10x** ($38.7M / $18.4M), not the 1.70x presented in the CIM using management's Adjusted EBITDA.

**Risk:** While 2.10x is within the 3.50x covenant maximum, the headroom is narrower than the CIM implies. More importantly, the $5.4M gap between bank EBITDA and management EBITDA means that any acquisition financing underwritten on management's Adjusted EBITDA will face a different covenant baseline than the existing lender uses. Buyer's lenders must be made aware of this distinction.

**Recommended Action:** Confirm covenant compliance on a bank-definition basis. Ensure acquisition financing term sheets are underwritten using the bank's EBITDA definition or a clearly negotiated alternative.

#### RF-05 | MEDIUM | Maintenance Capex Understated — Fleet Replacement Backlog of ~$9.8M

**Source Documents:** QofE Data Request Response (Fleet & Equipment Schedule; Capex Detail tabs)

**Finding:** The Fleet & Equipment Schedule discloses that **38 of 85 fleet and equipment assets (44.7%) are at or past their stated useful life**, with an average fleet age of 8.2 years against a weighted average useful life of 7.0 years. The estimated replacement cost for past-useful-life assets is **$9.8M**. Management's normalized maintenance capex estimate of $4.0M–$4.5M per year does not appear to account for this backlog. Spreading the $9.8M backlog over three years implies approximately **$3.3M/year in catch-up capex** above the stated run-rate — effectively doubling normalized maintenance capex.

**Risk:** The CIM's free cash flow presentation is based on $4.0M–$4.5M maintenance capex. If true normalized capex is $7.3M–$7.8M (including catch-up), free cash flow is overstated by approximately $3.3M annually, reducing implied free cash flow yield and debt service capacity.

**Recommended Action:** Commission an independent fleet appraisal and replacement schedule. Adjust free cash flow projections and acquisition financing models to reflect true normalized capex.

### CATEGORY 2: ENVIRONMENTAL & REGULATORY

#### RF-06 | CRITICAL | Stale Phase II ESA (2019) — Confirmed Contamination, Vapor Intrusion Unassessed, Cost Estimate Outdated

**Source Documents:** Phase II ESA Executive Summary; Real Property and Lease Schedule (§2.1); Environmental Permit Schedule (§2.1); Litigation and Regulatory Summary (§VI.A)

**Finding:** The only Phase II Environmental Site Assessment for the Company's owned Grand Junction headquarters (8750 Industrial Parkway) was completed in **June 2019 — over five years ago**. The ESA confirmed:

- **Petroleum hydrocarbon contamination** (TPH-diesel up to 4,200 mg/kg; benzene in groundwater at 12 µg/L, exceeding Colorado standards) in the former UST area.
- **Chlorinated solvent contamination** (PCE up to 18 mg/kg in soil; PCE at 22 µg/L and TCE at 8.7 µg/L in groundwater, both exceeding Colorado standards) in the former degreasing area.
- **Potential vapor intrusion** into the occupied 28,000 sq. ft. vehicle maintenance building (PCE at 450 µg/m³ and TCE at 120 µg/m³ in soil gas, both exceeding EPA RSLs). **No vapor intrusion assessment has been conducted.**
- **Groundwater plume containment unconfirmed** — the 2019 ESA recommended off-site sentinel wells that have not been installed.

The 2019 cost estimate of **$1.8M–$2.6M** is in 2019 dollars and explicitly noted by Elkhorn as requiring update before any transaction. The Company has accrued $2.2M for Grand Junction remediation — within the 2019 range but not updated for five years of inflation, potential plume migration, or the unassessed vapor intrusion pathway.

**Risk:** (1) Actual remediation costs may materially exceed the $2.2M accrual. (2) Vapor intrusion into an occupied building creates OSHA and tort liability exposure. (3) Off-site plume migration, if confirmed, could trigger additional regulatory notification and remediation obligations under Colorado Regulation No. 41, materially expanding scope and cost. (4) The Pollution Legal Liability policy **explicitly excludes gradual contamination at owned facilities** — meaning the confirmed contamination at Grand Junction is **uninsured**.

**Recommended Action:** Commission an updated Phase II ESA and vapor intrusion assessment immediately, as a condition of closing. Require the Company to install off-site sentinel monitoring wells. Obtain an independent remediation cost estimate in current dollars. Negotiate a specific environmental indemnity and/or escrow for Grand Junction remediation costs above the current accrual.

**Recommended Action:** Require resolution or escrow of the CDPHE matter before closing. Obtain a specific indemnity from Sellers for any penalty in excess of the Company's stated $200K–$450K settlement range. Confirm that the enforcement action will not affect the 2026 permit renewal.

#### RF-09 | HIGH | Salt Lake City Permit Not Transferred — Operating Under Pinnacle's Permit for 2+ Years

**Source Documents:** Environmental Permit Schedule (§2.2); Real Property and Lease Schedule (§4.1); Litigation and Regulatory Summary (§III.C)

**Finding:** Utah Hazardous Waste Transfer Station Permit UTH-0441 for the Salt Lake City facility remains in the name of **Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC** — not RES — more than two and a half years after the March 2022 acquisition. (2) The proposed acquisition of RES by Sycamore would constitute a further change in operational control, potentially requiring a new permit modification application. (3) The Salt Lake City facility serves approximately $16.2M of annual revenue (12.7% of total). Disruption to this facility's permit status could materially impair operations.

**Recommended Action:** Require completion of the Utah permit transfer as a condition of closing, or obtain a written comfort letter from the Utah Division of Waste Management confirming no enforcement action is contemplated. Assess whether the Sycamore acquisition triggers a new permit modification requirement.

#### RF-10 | CRITICAL | DOE IDIQ Ceiling Near Exhaustion — $12.1M Revenue Headwind in FY2025

**Source Documents:** Customer Contract Summary (DOE IDIQ Utilization Analysis tab; Customer Concentration Summary tab); CIM (§VI.D)

**Finding:** The DOE IDIQ Contract (DE-EM-0005523) generated $24.4M in FY2024 TTM revenue (19.2% of total). However, the IDIQ ceiling of $45.0M is **72.7% consumed** ($32.7M obligated) with only **$12.3M remaining** — yet the contract does not expire until August 31, 2026. At the current run rate of approximately $2.03M/month, the remaining ceiling will be **exhausted by approximately April–May 2025** — coinciding with the target closing date. After ceiling exhaustion, no new task orders can be issued under the existing contract without a ceiling modification.

**Risk:** If the ceiling is not increased (which requires a government contract modification and is not guaranteed), DOE revenue could decline from $24.4M (FY2024) to approximately $12.3M or less in FY2025 — a **$12.1M revenue headwind representing 9.5% of total revenue**. This risk is not disclosed in the CIM, which presents the DOE relationship as providing "substantial near-term revenue visibility." The LOI enterprise value is based on FY2024 TTM revenue and EBITDA that includes the full $24.4M DOE run rate.

**Additionally:** The contract is non-assignable under the Anti-Assignment Act (41 U.S.C. § 6305), and the contract's Special Contract Requirements (Clause H-12) require written notification to the Contracting Officer within 30 days of any change in ownership. Failure to notify may be grounds for termination for default.

**Recommended Action:** Immediately engage with the DOE Contracting Officer to assess the likelihood and timeline of a ceiling modification or follow-on contract award. Adjust the valuation model to reflect a downside scenario in which DOE revenue declines to $12.3M in FY2025. Ensure the required ownership change notification is provided to the Contracting Officer at or before closing.

#### RF-11 | HIGH | Salt Lake City Lease — Unresolved Assignment Creates Pre-Existing Default Risk

**Source Documents:** Real Property and Lease Schedule (§4.1, §6); Customer Contract Summary (Customer Concentration Summary tab); Litigation and Regulatory Summary (§V.B)

(2) The proposed Sycamore acquisition would constitute a **second change-of-control event** requiring a separate consent. (3) If the landlord refuses consent or declares a default, RES could lose the right to occupy the facility, disrupting approximately **$16.2M of annual revenue** (12.7% of total) and the operations of the Salt Lake City hazardous waste transfer station. (4) The facility also hosts the unresolved Utah permit (UTH-0441), compounding the risk.

**Recommended Action:** Obtain written landlord consent to both the existing Pinnacle-to-RES assignment and the proposed Sycamore acquisition as a condition of closing. If consent cannot be obtained, evaluate alternative facility options and the impact on revenue and operations.

#### RF-14 | HIGH | Asbestos Long-Tail Exposure — Pre-2015 Work Uninsured; 145 Sites Over 11 Years

**Source Documents:** Litigation and Regulatory Summary (§VI.B); Insurance Program Summary (§4); QofE Data Request Response (Service Line Detail tab)

**Finding:** RES operated an asbestos abatement service line from 2012 through September 30, 2023, performing work at approximately **145 commercial and industrial sites** across Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming. Claims arising from pre-2015 work would be uninsured.

**Recommended Action:** Require Sellers to obtain an asbestos-specific tail policy covering the 2012–2014 period as a condition of closing, or negotiate a specific indemnity with escrow support for asbestos-related claims. Obtain a legal opinion on the scope of potential exposure.

#### RF-17 | CRITICAL | $3.775M Change-of-Control Severance — $2.625M Single-Trigger, Unaddressed in LOI

**Source Documents:** Employment Agreements Summary (§2, §8); QofE Data Request Response (Executive Compensation tab); LOI (§4(c))

**Finding:** All five executive employment agreements define "Change of Control" to include a stock sale of greater than 50% of outstanding equity — directly triggered by the proposed 100% acquisition. Total aggregate severance: **$3.775M**:

- Derek Calloway (CEO): **$1.3125M — single trigger** (payable within 30 days of closing regardless of continued employment)
- Jason Calloway (COO): **$1.3125M — single trigger** (same terms)
- Patricia Engel (CFO): $425K — double trigger
- Kurt Dillon (VP Ops): $375K — double trigger
- Sandra Oakes (VP BD): $350K — double trigger

The LOI's definition of "Transaction Expenses" does not explicitly address change-of-control severance payments. The Employment Agreements Summary notes that "the treatment of change-of-control severance obligations under the proposed transaction's purchase price mechanism — including whether such amounts constitute 'Transaction Expenses' — should be confirmed by the parties' respective counsel." No such confirmation has been provided.

**Risk:** (1) The $2.625M single-trigger severance for the Calloway brothers is a certain cash obligation at closing, regardless of whether they continue in their roles. (2) If classified as Transaction Expenses, the full $3.775M reduces the Purchase Price dollar-for-dollar. If not classified as Transaction Expenses, the obligation falls on the Company post-closing, reducing free cash flow. (3) The Credit Agreement's change-of-control provision requires full repayment of $38.7M in debt (plus a $263K prepayment premium) within 5 business days of closing — a separate, concurrent cash obligation.

**Recommended Action:** Explicitly address the classification and payment mechanics of change-of-control severance in the SPA. Confirm whether the $3.775M is included in the LOI's Transaction Expenses definition. Ensure the acquisition financing accounts for both the debt repayment ($38.7M+) and the severance obligations ($3.775M) as day-one cash requirements.
