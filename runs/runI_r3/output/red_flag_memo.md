## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This memorandum presents a comprehensive analysis of material risks and red flags identified during the review of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES" or "Company") data room documents in connection with the proposed acquisition by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP. 

**Twenty (20) material red flags have been identified**, spanning financial, operational, regulatory, legal, and environmental categories. Of these:

- **4 CRITICAL** risks requiring immediate resolution before closing
- **8 HIGH** risks requiring significant attention and mitigation
- **6 MEDIUM** risks requiring management and monitoring
- **2 LOW** risks requiring standard due diligence follow-up

The aggregate financial exposure from identified contingencies and liabilities ranges from approximately **$800,000 to $2.5 million** (excluding the Grand Junction environmental remediation accrual of $3.2 million, which is already reserved). Several risks carry unquantifiable but potentially material exposure.

---

## CRITICAL RISKS (IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED)

### RF-001: DOE Contract Ceiling Exhaustion – $12.1M+ Revenue at Risk

**Issue:** The U.S. Department of Energy IDIQ contract (DE-EM-0005523) represents 19.2% of trailing twelve-month revenue ($24.4M). The contract has a $45M ceiling, of which 72.7% ($32.7M) has been consumed through September 30, 2024. Only $12.3M remains available.

**Critical Problem:** At the current run rate of approximately $24.4M annually ($2.03M/month), the remaining ceiling will be exhausted by April-May 2025 – approximately 6 months from now. The contract does not expire until August 31, 2026, leaving 16 months of unfunded contract period.

**Financial Impact:** If the ceiling is not increased or a new contract awarded, DOE revenue could decline from $24.4M (FY2024) to approximately $12.3M or less in FY2025, representing a potential revenue headwind of $12.1M (9.5% of current total revenue). This would materially impact EBITDA and valuation.

**Buyer Risk:** The valuation is predicated on normalized EBITDA of $22.8M. A $12.1M revenue decline would reduce EBITDA by approximately $2.5M-$3.0M (assuming 20-25% EBITDA margin), reducing enterprise value by $20M-$27M at an 8-9x multiple.

**Recommended Action:**
- Immediate engagement with DOE Contracting Officer to discuss ceiling modification or new contract opportunities
- Obtain written confirmation of DOE's intent to increase ceiling or issue new contract
- Develop contingency revenue plan to offset potential DOE revenue decline
- Consider earn-out or price adjustment mechanism tied to DOE revenue performance

### RF-003: Debt Change-of-Control Prepayment Obligation – $39.2M+ Cash Requirement

**Issue:** The senior secured credit facility with High Plains National Bank ($38.7M outstanding as of September 30, 2024) contains a mandatory prepayment provision triggered by a change of control. The proposed 100% stock acquisition by Sycamore Capital would trigger this provision.

**Critical Problem:** 
- Full prepayment of all outstanding obligations required within 5 business days of closing
- Prepayment premium of 1.0% applies if closing occurs between January 17, 2025 and January 16, 2026 (estimated $263K based on current term loan balance)
- Total estimated prepayment obligation: **$39.2M-$39.3M** (principal + premium + accrued interest)
- No consent or waiver mechanism available; only alternatives are refinancing or payoff

**Buyer Risk:** Significant cash requirement at closing. If refinancing not arranged in advance, transaction cannot close. Refinancing risk if replacement lender unwilling to provide equivalent terms.

**Recommended Action:**
- Immediately negotiate with High Plains National Bank regarding debt payoff/refinancing
- Obtain commitment letter from replacement lender for post-closing refinancing
- Evaluate whether seller should refinance debt pre-closing to eliminate change-of-control trigger
- Budget prepayment premium and refinancing costs in transaction economics
- Confirm debt payoff mechanics and timing in purchase agreement

---

### RF-007: Salt Lake City Lease Assignment – Unresolved Consent & $16.2M Revenue at Risk

**Issue:** The Salt Lake City transfer station lease (Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC) contains an explicit change-of-control clause requiring landlord consent for any assignment or change of control of the tenant. 

**Critical Problem:**
- Assignment from Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC to RES was requested on April 12, 2022 (nearly 3 years ago)
- **Landlord consent has NOT been obtained** despite nearly 3 years of occupancy by RES
- RES has been paying rent continuously, and landlord has accepted payments without objection
- The proposed Sycamore acquisition would trigger an ADDITIONAL change-of-control event, potentially requiring fresh consent
- Facility generates approximately $16.2M in annual revenue (12.7% of total revenue)

**Buyer Risk:** 
- Landlord could withhold consent to the Sycamore acquisition or demand lease modification/rent increase
- Facility disruption would materially impact operations and revenue
- Potential default under lease if consent not obtained
- Operational continuity at risk

**Recommended Action:**
- **BEFORE CLOSING:** Obtain written landlord consent to both (i) the Pinnacle-to-RES assignment and (ii) the Sycamore acquisition
- Negotiate lease amendment if landlord demands rent increase or other modifications
- Evaluate alternative facility options in Salt Lake City as contingency
- Confirm lease assignment status and landlord consent in representations & warranties
- Consider lease assumption/novation in purchase agreement

## HIGH-RISK ISSUES (SIGNIFICANT ATTENTION REQUIRED)

### RF-002: Customer Concentration – 67% of Revenue from Top 10 Customers

**Issue:** Top 10 customers represent 67% of trailing twelve-month revenue. Top 5 customers represent 50.4%. Top 2 customers (DOE + Consolidated Mining & Minerals Corp.) represent 30.6%.

**Problem:** 
- Consolidated Mining & Minerals Corp. MSA has 60-day termination for convenience clause
- Loss of either DOE or Consolidated Mining would materially impact revenue and profitability
- Limited customer diversification increases vulnerability to customer loss
- Commercial customer base (excluding DOE) is concentrated in mining and energy sectors, which are cyclical

**Financial Impact:** Loss of Consolidated Mining ($14.5M) would reduce revenue by 11.4% and EBITDA by approximately $2.5M-$3.0M, reducing enterprise value by $20M-$27M at an 8-9x multiple.

**Recommended Action:**
- Accelerate new customer acquisition in commercial segment
- Evaluate long-term contract opportunities with existing customers
- Develop customer retention strategy and relationship management plan
- Assess customer concentration risk in valuation and purchase price adjustment mechanisms

### RF-004: Environmental Liability – Grand Junction Facility Contamination

**Issue:** The Grand Junction headquarters facility (owned, $6.8M appraised value) has documented soil and groundwater contamination from historical industrial operations predating RES's 2009 acquisition.

**Problem:**
- Phase II ESA (June 2019) identified petroleum hydrocarbons and chlorinated solvents (TCE, PCE) in soil and groundwater
- Estimated remediation cost: $1.8M-$2.6M (2019 dollars)
- No updated ESA since June 2019 – conditions may have changed
- Vapor intrusion risk in occupied 28,000 sq. ft. vehicle maintenance building
- Potential off-site migration liability if plume extends beyond property boundary
- Company accrual of $3.2M may be inadequate if conditions have worsened

**Buyer Risk:**
- Actual remediation costs may exceed accrual
- Vapor intrusion assessment and mitigation could add $100K-$500K+ in costs
- Off-site groundwater contamination could trigger regulatory action and third-party claims
- Environmental liability could impair property value or limit future use

**Recommended Action:**
- Commission updated Phase II ESA prior to closing
- Conduct vapor intrusion assessment of occupied building
- Evaluate off-site monitoring well installation to confirm plume containment
- Confirm adequacy of $3.2M accrual; consider additional reserve if needed
- Obtain environmental representations & warranties in SPA
- Consider environmental liability insurance

### RF-005: Regulatory Enforcement – CDPHE Hazardous Waste Storage Violation

**Issue:** Colorado Department of Public Health & Environment (CDPHE) issued Notice of Violation (Docket CDPHE-HW-2024-0093) on July 18, 2024 for improper hazardous waste storage at the Grand Junction facility.

**Problem:**
- Company self-reported violation in June 2024 (positive factor)
- Dispute over violation period: Company asserts 12 days; CDPHE asserts 31 days
- Potential penalty range: $200K-$450K (company position) to $1.16M (CDPHE position)
- Matter is in administrative negotiation; settlement not yet finalized
- Unresolved regulatory matter creates contingent liability

**Buyer Risk:**
- Penalty exposure of $200K-$1.16M depending on violation period determination
- Regulatory compliance representation in SPA could be breached
- Precedent for future enforcement actions if compliance issues not resolved

**Recommended Action:**
- Obtain settlement agreement or penalty determination prior to closing
- Ensure adequate reserve for worst-case penalty scenario
- Obtain regulatory compliance representation in SPA
- Confirm corrective measures implemented to prevent recurrence
- Evaluate impact on RCRA permit renewal

## CONCLUSION

While Ridgeline Environmental Services represents an attractive platform with strong market positioning, recurring revenue characteristics, and experienced management, the identified red flags present material risks that require careful attention and mitigation before closing. The most critical issues – DOE contract ceiling exhaustion, debt change-of-control prepayment, Salt Lake City lease assignment, and change-of-control severance – must be resolved or adequately mitigated in the transaction structure.

The aggregate financial exposure from identified contingencies and liabilities is substantial and should be carefully factored into the purchase price and transaction structure. Buyer should consider earn-out provisions, price adjustments, and representations & warranties insurance to manage identified risks.
