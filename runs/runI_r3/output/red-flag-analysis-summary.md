## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This analysis identifies **20 material red flags** across financial, operational, regulatory, legal, and environmental categories in connection with the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES").

### Key Findings:

1. **DOE Contract Ceiling Exhaustion** – $12.1M revenue at risk in FY2025 if ceiling not increased
2. **Debt Change-of-Control Prepayment** – $39.2M+ cash requirement at closing
3. **Salt Lake City Lease Assignment** – Unresolved landlord consent for $16.2M revenue facility
4. **Change-of-Control Severance** – $3.775M certain liability to executives
5. **Customer Concentration Risk** – 67% of revenue from top 10 customers; 30.6% from top 2

**Aggregate Financial Exposure:** $67.5M-$70.5M (including transaction-specific costs and contingencies)

---

## CRITICAL RISKS (4 IDENTIFIED)

### 1. DOE Contract Ceiling Exhaustion – $12.1M+ Revenue Headwind

**Status:** CRITICAL – Immediate Action Required

The U.S. Department of Energy IDIQ contract (DE-EM-0005523) represents 19.2% of TTM revenue ($24.4M). The $45M ceiling is 72.7% consumed ($32.7M utilized), with only $12.3M remaining. At current run rate ($24.4M/year), the ceiling will be exhausted by April-May 2025, leaving 16 months of unfunded contract period through August 31, 2026.

**Impact:** Potential FY2025 revenue decline of $12.1M (9.5% of total revenue), reducing EBITDA by $2.5M-$3.0M and enterprise value by $20M-$27M at 8-9x multiple.

**Action:** Immediate DOE Contracting Officer engagement; obtain written confirmation of ceiling modification or new contract; develop contingency revenue plan.

---

### 2. Debt Change-of-Control Prepayment – $39.2M+ Cash Requirement

**Status:** CRITICAL – Immediate Action Required

Senior secured credit facility with High Plains National Bank ($38.7M outstanding) contains mandatory prepayment provision triggered by change of control. Full prepayment required within 5 business days of closing, with 1.0% prepayment premium ($263K) if closing occurs January 17, 2025 – January 16, 2026.

**Impact:** Total prepayment obligation of $39.2M-$39.3M (principal + premium + accrued interest). No consent or waiver mechanism available; only alternatives are refinancing or payoff.

**Action:** Immediately negotiate with High Plains National Bank; obtain commitment letter from replacement lender; budget prepayment premium in transaction economics.

---

### 3. Salt Lake City Lease Assignment – Unresolved Consent & $16.2M Revenue at Risk

**Status:** CRITICAL – Immediate Action Required

Salt Lake City transfer station lease (Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC) contains change-of-control clause requiring landlord consent. Assignment from Pinnacle to RES requested April 12, 2022 (nearly 3 years ago) but **consent NOT obtained**. Facility generates $16.2M annual revenue (12.7% of total).

**Impact:** Landlord could withhold consent to Sycamore acquisition or demand lease modification/rent increase. Facility disruption would materially impact operations and revenue.

**Action:** BEFORE CLOSING – Obtain written landlord consent to both Pinnacle-to-RES assignment and Sycamore acquisition; negotiate lease amendment if necessary; evaluate alternative facility options.

---

### 4. Change-of-Control Severance Obligations – $3.775M Certain Liability

**Status:** CRITICAL – Immediate Action Required

Five executive employment agreements contain change-of-control severance totaling $3.775M:
- Derek Calloway (CEO): $1,312,500 (single-trigger)
- Jason Calloway (COO): $1,312,500 (single-trigger)
- Patricia Engel (CFO): $425,000 (double-trigger)
- Kurt Dillon (VP Ops): $375,000 (double-trigger)
- Sandra Oakes (VP BD): $350,000 (double-trigger)

**Impact:** $2.625M certain severance at closing (Calloway brothers); additional $1.15M if other executives terminated within 12 months. Severance reduces purchase price or increases buyer's post-closing costs.

**Action:** Clarify severance treatment in purchase price mechanism; negotiate post-closing employment/retention agreements with founders; establish earn-out tied to performance.

## HIGH-RISK ISSUES (8 IDENTIFIED)

### 5. Customer Concentration – 67% of Revenue from Top 10 Customers

Top 10 customers = 67% of revenue; Top 5 = 50.4%; Top 2 (DOE + Consolidated Mining) = 30.6%. Consolidated Mining MSA has 60-day termination for convenience clause. Loss of either major customer would materially impact revenue and profitability.

**Action:** Accelerate new customer acquisition; evaluate long-term contracts with existing customers; develop customer retention strategy.

---

### 6. Environmental Liability – Grand Junction Facility Contamination

Grand Junction facility (owned, $6.8M appraised value) has documented soil and groundwater contamination from pre-2009 operations. Phase II ESA (June 2019) identified petroleum hydrocarbons and chlorinated solvents (TCE, PCE). Estimated remediation cost: $1.8M-$2.6M. No updated ESA since 2019. Vapor intrusion risk in occupied 28,000 sq. ft. building. Company accrual of $3.2M may be inadequate.

**Action:** Commission updated Phase II ESA; conduct vapor intrusion assessment; evaluate off-site monitoring wells; confirm adequacy of accrual.

---

### 7. Regulatory Enforcement – CDPHE Hazardous Waste Storage Violation

CDPHE Notice of Violation (Docket CDPHE-HW-2024-0093) issued July 18, 2024 for improper hazardous waste storage at Grand Junction facility. Company asserts 12-day violation period; CDPHE asserts 31 days. Potential penalty range: $200K-$450K (company position) to $1.16M (CDPHE position). Settlement negotiations ongoing.

**Action:** Obtain settlement agreement or penalty determination prior to closing; ensure adequate reserve; obtain regulatory compliance representation in SPA.

---

### 8. Litigation Risk – Ramirez Personal Injury Action

Miguel Ramirez (former field technician) filed personal injury action in U.S. District Court, District of Wyoming (Case 2:24-cv-00187) on August 12, 2024. Plaintiff alleges occupational exposure to VOCs/heavy metals without adequate PPE. Damages sought: $4.8M. Coverage determination pending with Frontier Specialty Insurance Group.

**Action:** Obtain coverage determination from insurer; evaluate settlement strategy; assess potential for similar claims; obtain litigation tail coverage.

---

### 9. Permit Transfer Status – Salt Lake City Facility

Salt Lake City transfer station permit (UTH-0441) remains in Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC name. Transfer application to RES pending with Utah Division of Waste Management since March 2022 (nearly 3 years). Ogden permit (UTH-0587) successfully transferred June 2022.

**Action:** Expedite permit transfer application; obtain written confirmation of transfer status; ensure buyer understands permit transfer timeline and risks.

---

### 10. EBITDA Adjustment Discrepancy – $35M-$58M Valuation Gap

Management Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M includes $5.4M in adjustments that do NOT qualify under bank's Consolidated EBITDA definition ($18.4M). Valuation gap of $35M-$58M depending on multiple applied. Adjustments include: owner comp normalization ($950K), litigation settlement ($1.2M), ERP costs ($650K), Pinnacle integration ($800K), asbestos wind-down ($1.8M).

**Action:** Prepare detailed support for each adjustment; benchmark against comparable transactions; negotiate adjustment treatment in SPA; consider conservative EBITDA baseline.

---

### 11. Founder Dependency – Key Person Risk

Derek Calloway (CEO, 32.5% owner) and Jason Calloway (COO, 32.5% owner) are co-founders and primary drivers of company strategy, customer relationships, and operations. Both have single-trigger change-of-control severance but no post-closing employment agreements finalized.

**Action:** Negotiate post-closing employment/retention agreements; establish earn-out tied to performance and continued employment; develop management succession plan.

## MEDIUM-RISK ISSUES (6 IDENTIFIED)

### 12. Fleet Age & Deferred Capex – $9.8M Replacement Backlog

44.7% of fleet (38 of 85 units) at or past stated useful life. Average fleet age 8.2 years vs. 7-year useful life. Estimated replacement cost for past-life units: $9.8M. Capex deferred during Pinnacle integration (FY2022-FY2023).

**Action:** Develop detailed fleet replacement plan; budget $3.3M+ annual capex for 3 years; conduct equipment condition assessment.

---

### 13. Asbestos Tail Liability – Unquantifiable Long-Tail Exposure

Company operated asbestos abatement service line FY2012-FY2023 (11 years) at ~145 sites. Two open workers' compensation claims from former employees alleging occupational respiratory exposure. Pollution liability policy retroactive date: January 1, 2015 (excludes pre-2015 work). No tail coverage procured.

**Action:** Obtain extended reporting period (tail) coverage; evaluate claims history; consider representations & warranties insurance.

---

### 14. Threatened Labor Action – NLRB Unionization Petition

NLRB election petition filed November 4, 2024 by International Brotherhood of Teamsters Local 455 covering 62 field technicians at Grand Junction and Casper facilities. Potential unionization of 12.8% of workforce.

**Action:** Monitor NLRB proceedings; assess potential impact on labor costs and operations; disclose to buyer.

---

### 15. Threatened Wage Claim – FLSA Overtime Violations

Demand letter received October 2024 from counsel representing ~8 former field technicians terminated in asbestos wind-down. Alleged FLSA overtime violations. Claimed exposure: $165K unpaid wages + $165K liquidated damages = $330K total. Potential wage claim exposure of $100K-$200K if lawsuit filed.

**Action:** Engage employment counsel to review classification and compensation records; evaluate settlement strategy; obtain wage/hour compliance representation in SPA.

---

### 16. Revenue Decline & Margin Compression – Growth Deceleration

FY2024 TTM revenue growth of 11.1% significantly below historical CAGR of 16.8% (FY2020-FY2024). EBITDA growth declined -27% YoY (reported EBITDA $18.4M vs. $25.2M prior year). Reported EBITDA margin compressed to 14.5% from 22%.

**Action:** Analyze root causes of growth deceleration and margin compression; develop management plan to restore growth trajectory; consider earn-out or price adjustment mechanism tied to future performance.

---

### 17. Insurance Coverage Gaps – Uninsured Liabilities

CGL policy contains blanket asbestos exclusion. Pollution liability policy excludes gradual contamination at owned facilities (Grand Junction soil/groundwater contamination not covered). Ramirez personal injury claim coverage determination pending.

**Action:** Obtain confirmation of insurance coverage for all known claims; evaluate representations & warranties insurance; disclose coverage gaps to buyer.

---

## LOW-RISK ISSUES (2 IDENTIFIED)

### 18. Lease Expiration – Pueblo Storage Yard

Pueblo Storage Yard lease expires April 30, 2025 (coincides with target closing date). Facility used for equipment/vehicle storage. Renewal terms under negotiation. No renewal options if not renewed.

**Action:** Finalize Pueblo lease renewal or identify alternative storage location; ensure resolution prior to closing.

---

### 19. Wyoming Permit Renewal – Casper Facility

Casper facility permit (WY-HW-2019-033) expired November 30, 2024. Renewal application timely filed October 15, 2024. Operating under timely renewal provisions. Expected approval Q1 2025.

**Action:** Monitor Wyoming DEQ for permit renewal decision; obtain written confirmation of renewal approval prior to closing.

---

### 20. Pinnacle Legal Status – Entity Consolidation

Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC acquired March 15, 2022 for $14.5M. Entity remains legally separate (not dissolved). Permits, leases, and operations integrated into RES but legal entity not consolidated.

**Action:** Clarify legal status of Pinnacle entity; determine whether dissolution or consolidation required; obtain representations regarding Pinnacle acquisition and integration.

## TRANSACTION STRUCTURE RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Purchase Price Adjustment:** Consider earn-out or price adjustment mechanism tied to DOE revenue performance
2. **Holdback/Escrow:** Increase holdback amount to 12-15% (vs. proposed 10%) to cover identified contingencies
3. **Representations & Warranties:** Obtain comprehensive reps & warranties with extended survival periods for environmental and regulatory matters
4. **Representations & Warranties Insurance:** Consider R&W insurance to cover identified risks
5. **Indemnification:** Negotiate specific indemnification provisions for DOE contract, environmental matters, and regulatory proceedings

---

## CONCLUSION

While Ridgeline Environmental Services represents an attractive platform with strong market positioning, recurring revenue characteristics, and experienced management, the identified red flags present material risks requiring careful attention and mitigation before closing. The four critical issues – DOE contract ceiling exhaustion, debt change-of-control prepayment, Salt Lake City lease assignment, and change-of-control severance – must be resolved or adequately mitigated in the transaction structure.

The aggregate financial exposure from identified contingencies and liabilities is substantial and should be carefully factored into the purchase price and transaction structure. Buyer should consider earn-out provisions, price adjustments, and representations & warranties insurance to manage identified risks.
