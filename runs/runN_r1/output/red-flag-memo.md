## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This memorandum summarizes material red flags and diligence concerns identified across thirteen data room documents reviewed in connection with the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES" or the "Company") by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP ("Buyer"). The proposed transaction is structured as a 100% stock acquisition at an indicative enterprise value of $182–$205 million (8.0x–9.0x Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M as presented by management).

Our review has identified **twenty-two (22) distinct red flags** spanning seven risk categories: (1) Financial / Quality of Earnings, (2) Environmental & Regulatory, (3) Debt & Change-of-Control, (4) Customer Concentration & Contract, (5) Real Property & Permitting, (6) Human Capital & Labor, and (7) Insurance & Liability. Several of these issues are individually material and, in combination, could warrant a meaningful reduction to the purchase price, restructuring of the indemnification framework, or additional pre-closing conditions.

**The three most critical issues requiring immediate attention are:**

1. **DOE IDIQ Ceiling Exhaustion** — The Company's largest customer (19.2% of revenue) is projected to exhaust its contract ceiling by April–May 2025, creating a potential $12.1M revenue headwind in FY2025 that is not reflected in the valuation.

2. **Adjusted EBITDA Discrepancy and Aggressive Add-Backs** — Management's Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M (CIM) vs. $23.8M (QoE data room) is internally inconsistent by $1.0M, and the bank's covenant EBITDA of $18.4M reflects that $4.4M–$5.4M of add-backs are non-qualifying under the credit agreement. The true normalized EBITDA may be materially lower than the valuation basis.

3. **Salt Lake City Lease — Dual Consent Failure** — The SLC transfer station lease (serving ~$16.2M of revenue) has an unresolved landlord consent from the 2022 Pinnacle acquisition, and the proposed Sycamore transaction would layer a second change-of-control event on top of the unresolved first. The facility also operates under a permit still held in Pinnacle's name.

## SECTION I: FINANCIAL / QUALITY OF EARNINGS

### RF-01 | EBITDA Discrepancy: CIM vs. QoE Data Room ($1.0M Gap — Unresolved)

**Severity: HIGH**

The CIM presents FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA of **$22.8M**, while the QoE data request response (EBITDA Bridge tab) calculates Adjusted EBITDA of **$23.8M** — a $1.0M discrepancy. Management acknowledges the gap in the QoE workbook but states the "specific adjustment category causing the discrepancy has not been identified." This is a significant red flag: the Company's own financial team cannot reconcile its two primary financial presentations to the Buyer. The valuation is anchored to Adjusted EBITDA, and a $1.0M discrepancy at an 8.0x–9.0x multiple implies a $8–9M swing in enterprise value. The QoE engagement by Bridgewater Forsyth Group must resolve this before any final valuation is agreed.

**Required Action:** Demand a written reconciliation of the $1.0M gap prior to LOI finalization. Confirm which figure ($22.8M or $23.8M) is the correct starting point for the QoE analysis.

### RF-02 | Aggressive and Potentially Non-Recurring EBITDA Add-Backs ($5.4M Total)

**Severity: HIGH**

Management's $5.4M in EBITDA adjustments (per QoE data room) include items that are questionable in nature or recurrence:

| Add-Back | Amount | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Litigation settlement (Clearwater Logistics) | $1.2M | This was a **receipt** (RES was the plaintiff), not a cost — adding back a one-time gain inflates EBITDA upward, which is non-standard and arguably misleading |
| Owner compensation normalization | $0.95M | Founders' salaries of $875K each are being normalized to $400K each; post-closing, if founders remain at $875K, this add-back evaporates |
| ERP implementation | $0.65M | Phase 2 described as "expected to be completed Q2 FY2025" — ongoing costs may not be fully non-recurring |
| Pinnacle integration costs | $0.80M | Integration described as "substantially complete" yet costs continue into FY2024 — 2.5 years post-close |
| Asbestos wind-down | $1.80M | Legitimate non-recurring, but asbestos tail liability exposure is uninsured pre-2015 (see RF-18) |

**Critically**, the credit agreement's Consolidated EBITDA definition **expressly excludes** all five of these add-back categories, capping qualifying non-recurring adjustments at $500K per year. The bank's EBITDA of $18.4M is the operative figure for covenant compliance — a 19% discount to management's $22.8M figure.

**Required Action:** Buyer's QoE team should independently validate each add-back. The owner compensation normalization is particularly sensitive — confirm post-closing employment terms before accepting this adjustment.

### RF-03 | Reported EBITDA Decline: FY2024 Reported EBITDA Down 27% YoY

**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

The QoE Historical P&L tab shows FY2024 Reported EBITDA of $26.0M (including D&A add-back) vs. $25.2M in FY2023 — but management's presentation of Reported EBITDA is $18.4M for FY2024, implying a **27% YoY decline** in reported earnings. This dramatic decline is masked by the $5.4M in add-backs. The underlying business generated significantly less cash earnings in FY2024 than in FY2023 on a reported basis, even as revenue grew 11%. This margin compression warrants investigation into whether cost inflation, project mix shift, or operational issues are structural.

**Required Action:** Obtain a detailed bridge from FY2023 to FY2024 reported EBITDA explaining the drivers of the decline. Assess whether the margin compression is temporary or structural.

### RF-04 | Fleet Capital Expenditure Underinvestment — Hidden Capex Backlog (~$9.8M)

**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

The Fleet & Equipment Schedule reveals that **38 of 85 fleet assets (44.7%) are at or past their stated useful life**, with an average fleet age of 8.2 years against a 7.0-year useful life. Many are marked "Replacement Deferred" or "At End of Life — No Replacement Scheduled." The estimated replacement cost for past-useful-life units alone is **$9.8M**. Management's normalized maintenance capex of $4.0–4.5M per year appears understated; spreading the $9.8M backlog over three years implies ~$3.3M/year in catch-up capex above the stated run-rate. True normalized maintenance capex may be $7.0–7.5M annually, reducing normalized free cash flow by approximately $3M/year.

**Required Action:** Commission an independent fleet condition assessment. Adjust normalized maintenance capex upward in the valuation model. Negotiate a capex true-up mechanism or purchase price reduction to reflect the deferred replacement backlog.

## SECTION II: ENVIRONMENTAL & REGULATORY

### RF-06 | Grand Junction Facility — Stale Phase II ESA (5+ Years Old) with Unresolved Contamination

**Severity: HIGH**

The Phase II ESA for the owned Grand Junction headquarters (8750 Industrial Parkway) was completed in **June 2019** — over five years ago. It identified:

- **Petroleum hydrocarbon contamination** (TPH-diesel up to 4,200 mg/kg; benzene in groundwater at 12 µg/L, exceeding the 5 µg/L Colorado standard) in the former UST area
- **Chlorinated solvent contamination** (PCE up to 18 mg/kg in soil; PCE at 22 µg/L and TCE at 8.7 µg/L in groundwater, both exceeding Colorado standards) in the former degreasing area
- **Vapor intrusion risk** — elevated PCE (450 µg/m³) and TCE (120 µg/m³) in soil gas adjacent to the occupied vehicle maintenance building, both exceeding EPA RSLs

The 2019 ESA explicitly recommended: (a) off-site sentinel monitoring wells (not yet installed), (b) a vapor intrusion assessment of the occupied maintenance building (not yet conducted), and (c) periodic re-assessment before any property transaction. **None of these recommendations have been implemented in five years.** The ESA's own cost estimate of $1.8–2.6M (2019 dollars) excludes vapor intrusion mitigation, off-site remediation, and natural resource damages — all of which could materially increase actual costs.

The Company has accrued $2.2M for Grand Junction remediation, but this is based on a stale 2019 estimate that explicitly warned against reliance in connection with a property transaction. Actual costs could be substantially higher, particularly given DNAPL migration risk for chlorinated solvents.

**Required Action:** Commission an updated Phase II ESA and vapor intrusion assessment immediately. Do not close without current environmental data. Negotiate a specific environmental indemnity with a higher cap and longer survival period (the LOI contemplates 6-year survival for environmental reps, which is appropriate). Consider a separate environmental escrow.

### RF-07 | CDPHE Enforcement Action — Active RCRA Violation (Docket CDPHE-HW-2024-0093)

**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

CDPHE issued a Notice of Violation on July 18, 2024 alleging improper storage of ~14,000 gallons of F001/F002 listed hazardous waste at the Grand Junction facility. The parties dispute the violation period: RES claims 12 days (max penalty $450K); CDPHE claims 31 days (max penalty $1.16M). No accrual has been recorded. The matter is in administrative negotiation with a settlement conference expected in Q1 2025 — potentially concurrent with or after the target signing date of February 14, 2025.

This is an active RCRA enforcement action against the Company's primary permitted facility. A RCRA violation can, in extreme cases, affect permit renewal or standing. The LOI specifically calls out this matter for "specific indemnification treatment."

**Required Action:** Require resolution or a specific indemnity escrow before closing. Obtain CDPHE's current settlement position. Confirm no permit modification or suspension risk. Ensure the $200K–$1.16M exposure range is fully covered by the indemnification framework.

### RF-08 | Wyoming Permit Expired — Operating Under Timely Renewal Provisions

**Severity: MEDIUM**

The Wyoming DEQ hazardous waste permit (WY-HW-2019-033) for the Casper facility **expired November 30, 2024**. The Company filed a renewal application on October 15, 2024 and is operating under "timely renewal provisions." While this is a recognized legal mechanism under Wyoming administrative law, it creates uncertainty: (a) the renewal is not guaranteed, (b) the Wyoming DEQ could impose new conditions, and (c) the credit agreement lists permit revocation as an Event of Default. The Casper facility serves approximately $30M of Wyoming revenue.

**Required Action:** Obtain written confirmation from Wyoming DEQ of the timely renewal status and expected timeline. Make permit renewal a closing condition. Confirm no material new conditions are expected.

### RF-09 | Utah Salt Lake City Permit — Still Held in Pinnacle's Name (2.5 Years Post-Acquisition)

**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

The Utah hazardous waste transfer station permit (UTH-0441) for the Salt Lake City facility remains in the name of **Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC** — 2.5 years after RES acquired Pinnacle. The transfer application was initiated but remains pending. The facility is being operated by RES personnel under Pinnacle's permit. While Pinnacle is a wholly-owned subsidiary, this creates regulatory risk: Utah Administrative Code R315-3 requires Division approval for ownership/control changes before they become effective. The proposed Sycamore acquisition would add another layer of change-of-control complexity to an already unresolved permit transfer.

**Required Action:** Accelerate the Utah permit transfer to completion before closing. Obtain written confirmation from the Utah Division of Waste Management that operations under the Pinnacle permit are compliant. Make permit transfer a closing condition.

### RF-10 | Asbestos Abatement — Uninsured Pre-2015 Tail Liability

**Severity: MEDIUM**

RES operated an asbestos abatement service line from 2012 through September 30, 2023, performing work at approximately 145 commercial and industrial sites across CO, UT, and WY. The current Pollution Legal Liability policy has a **retroactive date of January 1, 2015**, meaning claims arising from work performed from 2012 through 2014 are **not covered** by the current policy. The QoE Service Line Detail tab also discloses two open workers' compensation claims from former asbestos abatement employees alleging occupational respiratory exposure. The CGL policy contains a **blanket asbestos exclusion** with no tail coverage procured for completed operations.

Management assesses future asbestos claims as "Remote," but the long latency of asbestos-related disease (mesothelioma can manifest 20–50 years post-exposure) means this tail is genuinely long. No reserve has been established.

**Required Action:** Obtain a legal opinion on the scope of pre-2015 asbestos exposure. Consider requiring the sellers to procure a specific asbestos tail policy or establish a dedicated escrow. Negotiate an extended survival period for asbestos-related indemnification claims.

## SECTION III: DEBT & CHANGE-OF-CONTROL

### RF-11 | Credit Agreement Change-of-Control — Mandatory Prepayment of $38.7M+ Within 5 Business Days

**Severity: HIGH**

The High Plains National Bank credit agreement contains a **Change of Control** provision that is triggered by the proposed 100% stock acquisition. Upon a Change of Control, the Borrower must prepay **all outstanding obligations within five (5) business days** — approximately $38.7M in principal plus accrued interest (~$200–300K) plus a **1.0% prepayment premium on the term loan** (~$263K), for a total estimated mandatory prepayment of **$39.2–39.3M**. The credit agreement provides **no consent or waiver mechanism** — the only path is a payoff letter or refinancing.

This is a known, quantified closing cost that must be reflected in the purchase price mechanics. The LOI's Net Debt adjustment captures the principal balance, but the prepayment premium and breakage costs must also be included.

**Required Action:** Confirm the prepayment premium calculation based on the actual closing date. Ensure the LOI/SPA definition of "Net Debt" explicitly includes the prepayment premium and all breakage costs. Arrange committed refinancing before signing.

### RF-12 | Bank EBITDA vs. Management EBITDA — Covenant Leverage Ratio Discrepancy

**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

The credit agreement's Consolidated EBITDA definition produces $18.4M (bank definition) vs. management's $22.8M–$23.8M. The bank's leverage ratio is 2.10x ($38.7M / $18.4M), not the 1.70x implied by management's adjusted EBITDA. While the Company is within the 3.50x covenant maximum, the discrepancy is material: it means the Company's true leverage on a bank-covenant basis is 24% higher than management represents. If EBITDA were to decline (e.g., due to DOE ceiling exhaustion), covenant headroom could erode rapidly.

**Required Action:** Model covenant compliance under a downside scenario reflecting DOE revenue decline. Confirm the Company has not been in technical default at any point during the credit agreement term.

### RF-13 | Derek Calloway "Key Man" Provision in Credit Agreement

The credit agreement contains a **change-of-management covenant** requiring Derek Calloway to remain as CEO and devote substantially full-time attention to the business. ## SECTION IV: CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION & CONTRACT RISK

### RF-14 | DOE IDIQ Ceiling Exhaustion — $12.1M Revenue Headwind in FY2025

**Severity: CRITICAL**

The DOE IDIQ contract (DE-EM-0005523) is the Company's largest customer at $24.4M (19.2% of revenue). The contract has a $45.0M ceiling value, of which **$32.7M has been consumed** — leaving only **$12.3M** of remaining ceiling. At the current run rate of ~$24.4M/year (~$2.03M/month), the remaining ceiling will be **exhausted by approximately April–May 2025** — coinciding with the target closing date. The contract does not expire until August 31, 2026, meaning there could be **16 months of unfunded contract period** after ceiling exhaustion.

If the ceiling is not increased through a contract modification, FY2025 DOE revenue could decline from $24.4M to $12.3M or less — a **$12.1M revenue headwind representing 9.5% of total revenue**. At an 8.0x–9.0x EBITDA multiple, this could imply a $15–25M reduction in enterprise value (assuming ~30% flow-through to EBITDA). This risk is not disclosed in the CIM and is buried in the QoE customer data.

Additionally, the contract is **non-assignable** under the Anti-Assignment Act (41 U.S.C. § 6305), and the contract's Section H, Clause H-12 requires notification to the Contracting Officer within 30 days of any change in ownership. Failure to notify could be grounds for termination for default.

**Required Action:** Immediately engage with the DOE Contracting Officer regarding ceiling modification prospects. Obtain a written assessment of the likelihood and timeline for a ceiling increase or follow-on contract. Adjust the valuation model to reflect a downside scenario with no ceiling increase. Make DOE contract continuity a closing condition.

## SECTION V: REAL PROPERTY & PERMITTING

### RF-18 | Salt Lake City Lease — Dual Consent Failure; Potential Pre-Existing Default

**Severity: HIGH**

The Salt Lake City transfer station lease (2100 West North Temple) is the Company's most complex real property issue:

1. **Unresolved 2022 Assignment**: RES requested landlord consent to the Pinnacle-to-RES assignment on April 12, 2022. As of December 2024 — **32 months later** — no written consent has been received. The lease explicitly requires prior written consent for any change of control of the tenant.

2. **Second Change-of-Control Event**: The proposed Sycamore acquisition would constitute a second change-of-control event under the lease, requiring a separate consent — layered on top of the unresolved first.

3. **Landlord Inquiry**: In November 2024, the landlord (Wasatch Industrial Properties) sent correspondence specifically inquiring about the change of control and referencing the consent clause — suggesting the landlord is aware of the issue and may be positioning to assert rights.

4. **Revenue at Risk**: The SLC facility serves approximately **$16.2M of annual revenue** (Wasatch Front Petroleum $6.4M, Silver Creek Mining $5.8M, Utah DEQ $4.6M partial, Summit County $2.9M partial, Uinta Basin $0.2M, Bear River Chemical $1.7M).

5. **Permit Linkage**: The SLC facility also operates under a permit still held in Pinnacle's name (see RF-09), compounding the risk.

**Required Action:** Obtain landlord consent to both the 2022 assignment and the proposed Sycamore transaction before closing. This is a non-negotiable closing condition. Engage directly with Wasatch Industrial Properties immediately. If consent cannot be obtained, assess alternative facility options and the cost of relocating operations.

## SECTION VI: HUMAN CAPITAL & LABOR

### RF-21 | NLRB Unionization Petition — 62 Field Technicians (Filed November 4, 2024)

**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH**

The Employee Benefits Summary discloses (in a footnote) that the **International Brotherhood of Teamsters Local 455 filed an NLRB election petition on November 4, 2024** covering 62 field technicians at the Grand Junction and Casper facilities. This is a material development that is:

- **Not disclosed in the CIM** (which affirmatively states the workforce is "entirely non-unionized")
- Disclosed only in a footnote to the benefits summary, not in the litigation summary
- Potentially affecting the Company's largest operational facilities

If the union election succeeds, it could result in: (a) increased labor costs through collective bargaining, (b) work rules that reduce operational flexibility, (c) potential strikes or work stoppages, and (d) a credit agreement Event of Default if a labor dispute lasts more than 10 business days. The 62 affected employees represent approximately 13% of the total workforce and a significant portion of field operations at the two primary facilities.

**Required Action:** Obtain a full briefing on the NLRB petition, the Company's response strategy, and the election timeline. Assess the likelihood of a union victory and the potential cost impact of a collective bargaining agreement. The CIM's representation that the workforce is "non-unionized" may need to be qualified in the SPA disclosure schedules.

This inconsistency requires resolution.

**Required Action:** Confirm the trigger structure for all five executives. Ensure the SPA explicitly addresses the treatment of CoC severance payments in the purchase price mechanics. Budget $2.625M as a certain closing cost and up to $1.15M as a contingent post-closing cost.

## SECTION VII: INSURANCE & LIABILITY

### RF-23 (Additional) | Ramirez Personal Injury Claim — Coverage Reservation of Rights

**Severity: MEDIUM**

The *Ramirez v. RES* personal injury action (D. Wyo., Case No. 2:24-cv-00187) seeks $4.8M for alleged VOC and heavy metal exposure at a Sheridan, Wyoming remediation site. The insurer (Frontier Specialty) has issued a **reservation of rights letter** dated September 19, 2024, meaning coverage is not confirmed. If the claim falls under the pollution liability policy's gradual contamination exclusion (which excludes conditions not discovered and reported within 72 hours), coverage could be denied entirely. Management's estimated exposure of $500K–$1.5M assumes coverage; the uninsured exposure could be the full $4.8M claimed.

**Required Action:** Obtain the reservation of rights letter and assess the coverage position. Ensure the SPA indemnification framework covers the full uninsured exposure. No accrual has been recorded; consider requiring a reserve or escrow.
