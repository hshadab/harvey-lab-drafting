## Top Deal-Critical Findings

1. **EBITDA is overstated and internally inconsistent.** Management's $22.8M (CIM) vs. $23.8M (QofE) discrepancy is unresolved. The bank's credit agreement disallows all $5.4M in add-backs, yielding bank EBITDA of $18.4M. Buyer-adjusted EBITDA is ~$20.1M, implying EV of $141M–$181M vs. the LOI's $182M–$205M range.

2. **The DOE contract — 19.2% of revenue — faces a ceiling cliff.** At the current run rate, the $45M IDIQ ceiling will be exhausted by April–May 2025 (the target closing date), leaving ~16 months of unfunded contract period and a potential $12.1M FY2025 revenue headwind.

3. **The Salt Lake City facility is in technical default.** The lease assignment from Pinnacle to RES has been unconsented for 2.5 years. The Sycamore acquisition would layer a second change-of-control event on top of the unresolved prior assignment. The facility serves $16.2M of annual revenue and hosts a permit still in Pinnacle's name.

4. **The Phase II ESA is 5+ years old and the consultant explicitly recommended updating it before any transaction.** Vapor intrusion into the vehicle maintenance building was identified but never assessed. Off-site groundwater migration is unconfirmed. The 2019 cost estimate of $1.8M–$2.6M excludes vapor intrusion mitigation and is in 2019 dollars.

5. **The NLRB union petition was buried in a footnote.** A Teamsters election petition covering 62 field technicians at Grand Junction and Casper was filed November 4, 2024 — not disclosed in the CIM or litigation summary, despite the CIM citing non-union status as a competitive advantage.
