## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This memorandum summarizes the material red flags identified during our review of the Project Ridgeline data room for the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES" or the "Company") by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP ("Buyer"). The proposed transaction is structured as a 100% stock acquisition at an indicative enterprise value of $182M–$205M (8.0x–9.0x management's stated Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M).

**Our review identified 30 discrete red flags across 8 categories.** The issues range from a fundamental discrepancy in the EBITDA figure underpinning the valuation, to an active federal regulatory enforcement action, to a near-term revenue cliff in the Company's largest customer relationship. Several issues are, in our view, **deal-critical** and must be resolved before the Buyer can proceed to signing with confidence.

**Summary by Severity:**

| Severity | Count | Key Themes |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | 5 | EBITDA discrepancy; DOE ceiling exhaustion; SLC lease default; CDPHE enforcement; CoC severance |
| **High** | 7 | Stale Phase II ESA; permit in Pinnacle name; Wyoming permit expired; credit facility payoff; NLRB petition; multiple lease consents |
| **Medium** | 6 | Fleet replacement backlog; Ramirez litigation; insurance gaps; customer concentration; asbestos tail |
| **Low** | 2 | Dormant subsidiary; board governance |

**Bottom line:** The Company is a fundamentally sound environmental services platform with real competitive advantages. However, the data room reveals a pattern of deferred maintenance — regulatory, physical, and legal — that creates material risk for a buyer. The valuation must be stress-tested against a scrubbed EBITDA figure, and several issues require resolution as conditions precedent to closing.

## SECTION I: FINANCIAL AND QUALITY OF EARNINGS

### RF-01 | CRITICAL — Adjusted EBITDA Discrepancy: CIM vs. QofE Schedule ($1.0M Gap)

The Confidential Information Memorandum states Adjusted EBITDA of **$22.8M**. The QofE data package EBITDA Bridge tab calculates Adjusted EBITDA of **$23.8M** — a **$1.0M unexplained difference**. Management acknowledges the discrepancy and states it "intends to reconcile" but has not identified the specific adjustment causing it. This is a fundamental data integrity issue at the core of the valuation. At an 8.0x–9.0x multiple, a $1.0M EBITDA discrepancy implies an $8M–$9M valuation impact.

**Buyer Action:** Require full reconciliation before confirming the LOI price. Use the lower figure ($22.8M) pending resolution. The LOI's Enterprise Value range should be conditioned on QofE findings per Section 3.2.

### RF-02 | CRITICAL — Aggressive EBITDA Add-Backs: $5.4M Total; Bank Allows $0

Management adds back $5.4M to reach Adjusted EBITDA from reported EBITDA of $18.4M. Each add-back is problematic:

- **$1.2M "Litigation Settlement"**: This is a **receipt** of cash from Clearwater Logistics — income, not an expense. Adding it back to EBITDA is directionally incorrect as presented; it inflates Adjusted EBITDA by treating income as a non-recurring cost.
- **$950K Owner Compensation Normalization**: The founders are paid $875K each ($1.75M total) vs. a claimed market rate of $400K each ($800K). The $950K adjustment assumes replacement executives can be hired at $400K — a figure that may be understated for a $127M revenue environmental services platform.
- **$800K Pinnacle Integration Costs**: The Pinnacle acquisition closed March 2022 — 2.5 years ago. Integration costs that persist this long are arguably recurring, not one-time.
- **$1.8M Asbestos Wind-Down**: The asbestos service line had negative contribution margins in FY2023 (-20%) and generated $0 revenue in FY2024. Wind-down costs are legitimate, but the service line's deterioration over multiple years suggests this was a structural problem, not a one-time event.
- **$650K ERP Implementation**: The QofE data package notes that Phase 2 of the ERP implementation is expected to be completed in Q2 FY2025 — meaning costs are ongoing.

Critically, the Company's credit agreement with High Plains National Bank **explicitly disallows all five categories** as EBITDA add-backs, yielding bank-defined Consolidated EBITDA of approximately **$18.4M**. At the LOI's 8.0x–9.0x multiple, the bank EBITDA implies an enterprise value of $129M–$166M — well below the $182M–$205M LOI range.

**Buyer Action:** Commission independent QofE; challenge each add-back; use bank EBITDA as the floor for valuation sensitivity. The Buyer's adjusted EBITDA, applying 50% haircuts to the more defensible add-backs, is approximately **$20.1M**, implying an enterprise value of $141M–$181M at 7.0x–9.0x.

### RF-03 | HIGH — Revenue Deceleration and Organic Growth Concerns

Revenue growth has decelerated from 19.0% (FY2022) to 17.2% (FY2023) to **11.1% (FY2024 TTM)**. More concerning, reported EBITDA fell **27% year-over-year** (FY2023: $25.2M → FY2024: $18.4M). The Industrial Cleaning service line's 48% revenue spike in FY2024 is partly attributable to absorbing former asbestos abatement clients — a one-time benefit that will not recur. The "All Other Customers" revenue cohort declined 30.7% year-over-year, suggesting the long tail of smaller accounts is eroding.

**Buyer Action:** Obtain FY2025 budget and pipeline; stress-test revenue projections; assess sustainability of industrial cleaning growth; consider earnout structure tied to FY2025 revenue performance.

### RF-04 | MEDIUM — Maintenance CapEx Understated; $9.8M Fleet Replacement Backlog

Management normalizes maintenance capex at $4.0M–$4.5M per year. However, the fleet schedule reveals that **38 of 85 assets (44.7%) are at or past their stated useful life**, with an estimated replacement cost of $9.8M for past-life units alone. Spreading this backlog over three years implies approximately $3.3M per year in catch-up capex above the current run-rate. True normalized maintenance capex is likely **$6.5M–$7.5M per year**, reducing free cash flow by $2M–$3M versus management's presentation. Several assets are marked "At End of Life — No Replacement Scheduled," which is operationally and DOT-compliance concerning for a hazardous waste transporter.

**Buyer Action:** Commission independent fleet inspection; adjust normalized capex in valuation model; negotiate purchase price reduction or escrow for deferred fleet maintenance.

## SECTION II: ENVIRONMENTAL AND REGULATORY

### RF-05 | CRITICAL — Active CDPHE RCRA Enforcement Action at Grand Junction

The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment issued a Notice of Violation (Docket No. CDPHE-HW-2024-0093) on July 18, 2024, alleging unauthorized hazardous waste storage at the Company's Grand Junction RCRA Part B facility. The parties dispute the violation period: the Company claims 12 days (maximum penalty $450K); CDPHE claims 31 days (maximum penalty $1.16M). **No accrual has been recorded.** A settlement conference is expected in Q1 2025.

This is not merely a financial exposure. A RCRA violation at the Company's primary permitted facility — the cornerstone of its regulatory moat — creates reputational risk with the DOE and state regulators, and could result in permit conditions that restrict operations. The credit agreement also treats any environmental remediation order exceeding $1M as an Event of Default.

**Buyer Action:** Require resolution or escrow before closing; obtain CDPHE status update; assess whether the violation affects DOE performance ratings; negotiate specific indemnity with 6-year survival.

### RF-06 | CRITICAL — Stale Phase II ESA (5+ Years Old); Vapor Intrusion Unassessed

The only Phase II Environmental Site Assessment for the owned Grand Junction property is dated **June 2019** — over five years old. The ESA consultant (Elkhorn Environmental Consultants) **explicitly recommended updating the assessment before any property transaction**. Key unresolved issues include:

- **Vapor intrusion pathway**: Soil gas sampling detected PCE at 450 µg/m³ and TCE at 120 µg/m³ adjacent to the 28,000 sq ft vehicle maintenance building — both exceeding EPA Regional Screening Levels. A vapor intrusion assessment was recommended but never conducted. Building occupants (fleet maintenance personnel) may be exposed to chlorinated solvent vapors daily.
- **Off-site groundwater migration**: No off-site sentinel wells have been installed. The ESA concluded the plume appeared contained, but noted this conclusion was "preliminary" given the limited monitoring network. The Colorado River is 1.2 miles downgradient.
- **DNAPL migration risk**: PCE and TCE are dense non-aqueous phase liquids that can migrate vertically through aquifers. The ESA noted significant cost uncertainty due to potential DNAPL migration below the investigation depth.
- **Stale cost estimate**: The 2019 estimate of $1.8M–$2.6M is in 2019 dollars and explicitly excludes vapor intrusion mitigation, off-site remediation, natural resource damages, and inflation.

**Buyer Action:** Commission updated Phase II ESA and vapor intrusion assessment immediately as a condition precedent to signing. Do not close without current environmental data. Negotiate separate environmental escrow above the current $2.2M accrual.

### RF-07 | HIGH — Grand Junction Remediation Accrual Potentially Inadequate

The Company's $2.2M Grand Junction remediation accrual (within a $3.2M total) is based on the 2019 cost estimate that explicitly excludes vapor intrusion assessment and mitigation, off-site groundwater remediation, natural resource damages, and inflation. The Buyer's updated assessment, pending a new Phase II ESA, suggests potential remediation costs of **$3M–$6M+**. The accrual shortfall could be $800K–$3.8M or more.

**Buyer Action:** Require updated cost estimate; negotiate environmental escrow above current accrual; obtain environmental impairment liability coverage or specific indemnity.

### RF-08 | MEDIUM — Asbestos Long-Tail Liability; Pre-2015 Coverage Gap

RES operated asbestos abatement at approximately 145 commercial and industrial sites from 2012 through September 2023. The current Pollution Legal Liability policy has a **retroactive date of January 1, 2015**, leaving work performed from 2012 through 2014 without pollution liability coverage. The CGL policy contains a **blanket asbestos exclusion**. No completed operations tail coverage has been procured. Two workers' compensation claims from former asbestos employees remain open. Asbestos-related latency periods mean claims could emerge 10–40 years post-exposure.

**Buyer Action:** Require asbestos-specific completed operations tail coverage as a closing condition; negotiate specific indemnity for pre-2015 asbestos claims; review open workers' compensation claims.

## SECTION III: DOE CONTRACT AND REVENUE SUSTAINABILITY

### RF-09 | CRITICAL — DOE IDIQ Ceiling Near Exhaustion; $12M+ FY2025 Revenue Headwind

The DOE IDIQ contract (DE-EM-0005523, $45M ceiling) is the Company's largest customer relationship at $24.4M (19.2% of revenue). As of October 2024, **$32.7M (72.7%) of the ceiling has been consumed in 61.7% of the contract period**. At the FY2024 run rate of approximately $2.03M per month, the remaining $12.3M ceiling will be exhausted by approximately **April–May 2025** — coinciding with the target closing date. The contract does not expire until August 31, 2026, leaving approximately 16 months of unfunded contract period.

If the ceiling is not increased through a contract modification, FY2025 DOE revenue could decline from $24.4M to $12.3M or less — a **$12.1M revenue headwind representing 9.5% of total revenue**. At an 8.0x–9.0x multiple, this implies a $97M–$109M valuation impact on run-rate EBITDA. The CIM presents the DOE relationship as providing "substantial near-term revenue visibility" — a characterization that is materially misleading given the ceiling utilization data.

**Buyer Action:** Obtain written confirmation from the DOE Contracting Officer on ceiling modification status before signing. Model the downside scenario with DOE revenue at $12.3M. Consider structuring an earnout tied to DOE revenue or a purchase price adjustment if the ceiling is not modified pre-close.

### RF-10 | CRITICAL — DOE Contract Non-Assignability and Novation Risk

The DOE IDIQ contract is non-assignable per the Anti-Assignment Act (41 U.S.C. §6305) and FAR 42.12. Contract Clause H-12 requires written notification to the Contracting Officer within 30 days of any change in ownership. While a stock acquisition may not technically constitute an assignment of the contract, the DOE may require a novation agreement — a process that can take 3–6 months and requires execution by the Government, the original contractor, and the successor entity. Failure to notify could be grounds for termination for default. The LOI lists governmental approvals as a closing condition but does not specifically address DOE novation.

**Buyer Action:** Engage the DOE Contracting Officer immediately to determine novation requirements. Build the novation timeline into the deal schedule. Do not close without Contracting Officer acknowledgment.

## SECTION IV: REAL PROPERTY AND LEASES

### RF-11 | CRITICAL — Salt Lake City Lease: Unconsented Assignment Since March 2022

The Salt Lake City transfer station lease (2100 West North Temple, Salt Lake City, UT 84116) was originally executed by Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC. RES requested landlord consent to assignment from Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC on **April 12, 2022 — over 2.5 years ago**. No written consent has been received. The lease explicitly defines any change of control of the tenant as an assignment requiring prior written consent. RES has been in **technical default** since March 2022.

The proposed Sycamore acquisition would layer a **second change-of-control event** on top of the unresolved prior assignment. The SLC facility serves approximately **$16.2M of annual revenue (12.7% of total)**, including customers Wasatch Front Petroleum ($6.4M), Silver Creek Mining ($5.8M), Utah DEQ ($4.6M), and others. The facility also hosts the Utah hazardous waste permit UTH-0441, which remains in Pinnacle's name (see RF-14). The landlord has recently sent correspondence inquiring about the change of control — suggesting awareness of the issue.

**Buyer Action:** Obtain written landlord consent before closing as a condition precedent. If consent is refused, assess alternative facility options. Do not close without resolution.

### RF-12 | HIGH — Multiple Leases Require Landlord Consent for Sycamore Acquisition

Seven of ten leased facilities require landlord consent for assignment or change of control. Beyond the SLC issue, operationally critical leases at Casper (10-year term, $22K/month) and Billings (5-year term, $14.2K/month) require consent. Failure to obtain consents could result in lease terminations affecting key operational hubs.

**Buyer Action:** Begin landlord consent process immediately for all seven leases. Prioritize Casper and Billings. Build consent timeline into the deal schedule.

### RF-13 | MEDIUM — Pueblo Storage Yard Lease Expiring at Target Closing Date

The Pueblo, CO equipment storage yard lease expires **April 30, 2025** — the same date as the target closing. Renewal negotiations are underway but no terms have been agreed. If the lease is not renewed, equipment and vehicles must be relocated, incurring costs and operational disruption.

**Buyer Action:** Confirm renewal status before closing; if not renewed, assess relocation costs and include in purchase price adjustment.

## SECTION V: PERMITTING AND REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

### RF-14 | HIGH — SLC Transfer Station Permit (UTH-0441) Still in Pinnacle's Name

The Utah hazardous waste transfer station permit for the SLC facility (UTH-0441) remains in the name of **Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC** — 2.5 years after the acquisition. The transfer application is pending with the Utah Division of Waste Management. Under Utah Administrative Code R315-3, permit modifications require Division approval before becoming effective. Operating under a permit in a different entity's name creates regulatory compliance risk and could be challenged in connection with the proposed transaction. By contrast, the Ogden permit (UTH-0587) was successfully transferred in June 2022, demonstrating the process is achievable.

**Buyer Action:** Accelerate the permit transfer application; engage Utah DEQ proactively; assess whether the proposed transaction triggers additional permit review. Do not close without transfer or written Division confirmation.

### RF-15 | HIGH — Wyoming Casper Permit Expired; Operating Under Timely Renewal

The Wyoming hazardous waste permit (WY-HW-2019-033) for the Casper facility **expired November 30, 2024**. The renewal application was filed October 15, 2024. The facility is currently operating under Wyoming's timely renewal provisions. While legally permissible, this creates uncertainty: the renewal could be conditioned or denied; the proposed transaction could trigger additional regulatory review; and the credit agreement lists permit revocation as an Event of Default.

**Buyer Action:** Monitor renewal status closely; engage Wyoming DEQ regarding the transaction; obtain written confirmation of renewal timeline; build into deal schedule as a condition precedent.

## SECTION VI: DEBT AND CHANGE OF CONTROL

### RF-16 | HIGH — Mandatory Credit Facility Payoff: $39.2M+ with Prepayment Premium

The proposed acquisition triggers the Change of Control definition under the High Plains National Bank credit agreement (Calloway family ceasing to hold 51%; Sycamore acquiring more than 50%). This requires **mandatory prepayment of all obligations within five business days of closing**. The credit agreement provides **no waiver or consent mechanism** — the only option is payoff or refinancing. Total estimated payoff: approximately **$39.2M–$39.3M** (principal $38.7M + 1% prepayment premium on term loan $263K + accrued interest $200K–$300K + SOFR breakage costs TBD). The LOI's Net Debt adjustment captures the principal but may not fully account for the prepayment premium and breakage costs.

**Buyer Action:** Confirm payoff amount with High Plains National Bank; obtain payoff letter; ensure acquisition financing covers the full payoff including premium; include prepayment premium and breakage costs in the Net Debt definition in the SPA.

### RF-17 | MEDIUM — Derek Calloway "Key Man" Covenant in Credit Agreement

The credit agreement requires Derek Calloway to remain as CEO and devote substantially full-time attention to the business. His departure without a Lender-approved successor constitutes an Event of Default. While the facility will be repaid at closing, this covenant underscores the key-man dependency risk for the business and is relevant if any bridge or rollover financing is contemplated.

**Buyer Action:** Confirm Calloway's post-close commitment; negotiate employment agreement before closing; assess key-man life insurance.

## SECTION VII: EMPLOYMENT AND HUMAN CAPITAL

### RF-18 | CRITICAL — Single-Trigger Change-of-Control Severance: $2.625M Payable at Closing

Derek and Jason Calloway each have **single-trigger** change-of-control severance provisions entitling them to 18 months of base salary ($1,312,500 each) payable within 30 days of closing, **regardless of post-close employment status**. Total: **$2,625,000**. This is a certain cash outflow at closing. The LOI's Transaction Expenses definition does not explicitly address these payments. If classified as Transaction Expenses, they reduce the purchase price; if buyer-borne, they are an additional cost. The QofE data package flags this as unresolved. The three remaining executives (CFO, VP Operations, VP Business Development) have double-trigger provisions totaling $1,150,000 — contingent on post-close termination or material diminution of duties.

**Buyer Action:** Resolve classification in SPA negotiations. If seller-borne, deduct from purchase price. Negotiate new post-close employment agreements with the Calloway brothers to potentially restructure the severance obligation.

### RF-19 | HIGH — NLRB Union Election Petition: 62 Field Technicians

An NLRB election petition was filed **November 4, 2024** by the International Brotherhood of Teamsters Local 455, covering 62 field technicians at the Grand Junction and Casper facilities. This was disclosed only in a **footnote** to the Employee Benefits Summary — it does not appear in the CIM, the Litigation and Regulatory Summary, or any other primary data room document. The CIM prominently cites the Company's non-union status as a competitive advantage. If the union wins an election, it could result in collective bargaining, wage increases of 10–20% for 62 employees, work rule changes, and reduced operational flexibility.

**Buyer Action:** Obtain full details on the NLRB petition immediately; assess election timeline; evaluate labor counsel strategy; ensure disclosure in SPA representations; assess impact on operating costs. The failure to disclose this in the CIM or litigation summary is itself a red flag regarding disclosure completeness.

### RF-20 | MEDIUM — FLSA Wage Claim: Former Asbestos Employees

Eight former asbestos abatement employees filed a demand letter in October 2024 alleging unpaid overtime under the FLSA and Colorado COMPS Order. Claimed exposure: approximately $330K (including liquidated damages). No lawsuit has been filed. Management estimates exposure at $100K–$200K and cites the motor carrier exemption as a defense. The claim is connected to the asbestos wind-down, which management has characterized as fully resolved.

**Buyer Action:** Obtain employment counsel analysis; assess exemption applicability; consider pre-close settlement; ensure indemnity coverage in SPA.

## SECTION VIII: FLEET, CAPITAL EXPENDITURE, AND INSURANCE

### RF-21 | MEDIUM — Fleet Age and Deferred Replacement: Operational and DOT Risk

As detailed in RF-04, 38 of 85 fleet assets are at or past useful life. Several assets are marked "At End of Life — No Replacement Scheduled," including vacuum trucks, rolloff trucks, and a wheel loader. An aging fleet increases breakdown risk, maintenance costs, and DOT compliance risk. FMCSA out-of-service violations on hazardous waste transport vehicles can result in operational shutdowns and regulatory consequences.

### RF-22 | MEDIUM — Growth CapEx Elevated; "Other Growth Initiatives" Unexplained

Growth capex has averaged $3.9M per year over five years and was $4.5M in FY2024. The "Other Growth Initiatives" line jumped from $650K (FY2023) to $2.0M (FY2024) with limited explanation. If growth capex is reduced post-close, revenue growth may slow; if maintained, free cash flow is lower than management's normalized presentation suggests.

**Buyer Action:** Obtain detail on the $2.0M "Other Growth Initiatives" line; assess which growth capex is truly discretionary vs. required to maintain revenue; adjust the FCF model accordingly.

### RF-23 | MEDIUM — Ramirez Personal Injury Claim: $4.8M Sought; Coverage Uncertain

Miguel Ramirez (former field technician) filed suit in August 2024 alleging VOC and heavy metal exposure at a Sheridan, Wyoming remediation site, seeking $4.8M including punitive damages. The insurer issued a **reservation of rights letter** on September 19, 2024 — coverage determination is pending. If the CGL policy's pollution exclusion applies, or if the PLL policy's gradual contamination exclusion applies, the Company could bear the full exposure. No accrual has been recorded.

**Buyer Action:** Obtain coverage determination status from Frontier Specialty; assess litigation strategy; ensure indemnity covers uncovered exposure; allocate holdback accordingly.

### RF-24 | MEDIUM — OSHA Recordkeeping Violations: Potential Safety Record Overstatement

OSHA Region VIII found that RES failed to timely record three recordable injuries in calendar year 2023 at Grand Junction and Casper. While the financial penalty ($4,580) is minimal, the finding raises questions about whether the Company's TRIR of 2.8 (cited in the benefits summary) is accurate, and whether the safety record presented in the CIM is overstated. The Ramirez lawsuit also alleges inadequate PPE and air monitoring — consistent with a pattern of safety compliance gaps.

### RF-25 | MEDIUM — Pollution Legal Liability: Gradual Contamination Exclusion

The PLL policy explicitly excludes coverage for **gradual contamination at owned or operated facilities** — precisely the type of contamination documented at Grand Junction (petroleum hydrocarbons and chlorinated solvents from decades of prior operations). The Phase II ESA characterizes the contamination as "gradual releases over extended periods." This means the $2.2M+ Grand Junction remediation obligation is **entirely uninsured**.

### RF-26 | MEDIUM — Claims-Made PLL Policy: Tail Coverage Risk at Transaction

The PLL policy is written on a claims-made basis with a January 1, 2015 retroactive date. At closing, the policy must be continued, replaced, or a tail purchased (at 150% of the annual premium). If the policy lapses without a tail, claims arising from pre-close pollution conditions reported post-close will be uninsured.

**Buyer Action:** Engage Frontier Specialty immediately regarding the transaction; obtain tail coverage quote; include tail cost in Transaction Expenses; negotiate representations and indemnities.

There is no independent director.

## SECTION XI: DEAL STRUCTURE RECOMMENDATIONS

Based on our red flag analysis, we recommend the following deal structure modifications:

**1. Purchase Price Adjustment**

The LOI's indicative EV range of $182M–$205M is based on management's stated Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M at 8.0x–9.0x. Our analysis suggests the appropriate Buyer-adjusted EBITDA is approximately $20.1M, implying an EV range of approximately $141M–$181M at 7.0x–9.0x. We recommend:

- Conditioning the final Enterprise Value on QofE findings (already contemplated in LOI §3.2)
- Applying a multiple at the lower end of the range (7.5x–8.0x) given the revenue deceleration and DOE ceiling risk
- Negotiating a purchase price reduction of $10M–$20M to reflect identified risks

**2. Enhanced Escrow Structure**

The LOI contemplates a 10% holdback ($18.2M–$20.5M). We recommend:

- Maintaining the general holdback at 10% for 18 months
- Establishing a **separate environmental escrow** of $3M–$5M with a 6-year survival period to cover Grand Junction remediation and CDPHE penalty
- Establishing a **separate regulatory/contract escrow** of $2M–$3M for DOE contract and permit-related risks

**3. Specific Indemnities**

The following matters warrant specific indemnification provisions outside the general basket and cap:

- CDPHE enforcement action (CDPHE-HW-2024-0093)
- Grand Junction environmental remediation (above accrued amount)
- Asbestos long-tail claims (pre-2015 work)
- NLRB petition and related labor costs
- SLC lease default and landlord claims
- DOE contract termination or ceiling non-modification

**4. Conditions Precedent to Signing**

- Updated Phase II ESA and vapor intrusion assessment
- EBITDA reconciliation (CIM vs. QofE)
- DOE Contracting Officer engagement and novation determination

**5. Conditions Precedent to Closing**

- Written landlord consent for SLC lease (and cure of prior default)
- Utah permit transfer (UTH-0441) or Division written confirmation
- Wyoming permit renewal
- All material landlord consents (7 leases)
- DOE Contracting Officer acknowledgment
- High Plains National Bank payoff letter
- Calloway employment agreements executed
- PLL tail coverage procured

**6. Earnout Structure**

Consider a $10M–$15M earnout tied to FY2025 DOE revenue and total revenue performance, to align seller incentives with the DOE ceiling modification risk.
