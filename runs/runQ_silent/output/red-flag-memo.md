# RIDGELINE ENVIRONMENTAL SERVICES, INC.
## ACQUISITION DUE DILIGENCE RED FLAG ANALYSIS

**Project:** Project Ridgeline (Sycamore Capital Partners, LP)  
**Target Company:** Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc.  
**Prepared:** December 2024  
**Scope:** Comprehensive review of 13 data room documents

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This analysis identifies 20 material red flags across financial, operational, regulatory, and legal dimensions of the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP. The analysis is based on comprehensive review of 13 data room documents, including the Letter of Intent, financial statements, customer contracts, employment agreements, litigation summaries, environmental assessments, and regulatory filings.

### KEY FINDINGS:

- **4 CRITICAL** red flags requiring immediate resolution prior to closing
- **8 HIGH-severity** issues requiring detailed due diligence and mitigation planning
- **8 MEDIUM-severity** issues requiring monitoring and contingency planning

The most significant risks relate to: (1) DOE contract ceiling exhaustion creating $12.1M+ revenue headwind; (2) mandatory debt prepayment triggered by change of control; (3) unresolved lease assignment at Salt Lake City facility; and (4) environmental liabilities at Grand Junction headquarters.

**Estimated aggregate contingent liability exposure: $800K–$2.5M** (excluding Grand Junction environmental remediation and potential debt refinancing costs).

---

## CRITICAL RED FLAGS (4)

### RF-001: DOE Contract Ceiling Exhaustion Risk

The U.S. Department of Energy IDIQ contract (DE-EM-0005523) represents 19.2% of trailing twelve-month revenue ($24.4M of $127.3M total). The contract has a $45M ceiling, of which $32.7M has been utilized, leaving only $12.3M remaining. At the current run rate of approximately $24.4M annually, the remaining ceiling will be exhausted by April–May 2025, leaving approximately 16 months of unfunded contract period through the contract expiration date of August 31, 2026.

**IMPACT:** If the ceiling is not increased or a new contract is not awarded, DOE revenue could decline from $24.4M (FY2024) to approximately $12.3M or less in FY2025, representing a potential revenue headwind of $12.1M (9.5% of current total revenue). This would materially impact EBITDA and valuation.

**MITIGATION:** Immediate engagement with DOE Contracting Officer to discuss ceiling modification or new contract opportunities. Develop contingency revenue plan to offset potential DOE revenue decline.

---

### RF-003: Mandatory Debt Prepayment on Change of Control

The senior secured credit facility with High Plains National Bank ($38.7M outstanding as of September 30, 2024) contains a mandatory prepayment provision triggered by a change of control. The definition of change of control includes acquisition of 50% or more of outstanding equity, which would be triggered by the proposed 100% stock acquisition by Sycamore Capital.

The credit agreement requires full prepayment of all outstanding obligations within 5 business days of the change of control event. Additionally, a prepayment premium applies to the term loan based on the timing of prepayment: 1.0% if prepayment occurs between January 17, 2025 and January 16, 2026 (estimated premium: $263K based on current outstanding balance).

**IMPACT:** Total estimated mandatory prepayment obligation: $39.2M–$39.3M (including principal, accrued interest, and prepayment premium). This represents a significant cash requirement at closing and creates refinancing risk if the lender is unwilling to waive the change-of-control provision or if replacement financing cannot be secured on acceptable terms.

**MITIGATION:** Negotiate debt payoff or refinancing with High Plains National Bank prior to closing. Obtain commitment letter from replacement lender. Budget for prepayment premium in purchase price structure.

---

### RF-007: Salt Lake City Lease Assignment Consent Risk

The Salt Lake City transfer station facility (2100 West North Temple, Salt Lake City, UT 84116) is leased from Wasatch Industrial Properties, LLC under a commercial lease that contains an explicit change-of-control clause requiring landlord consent for any assignment or change of control of the tenant.

Following RES's acquisition of Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC in March 2022, an assignment request was submitted to the landlord on April 12, 2022. As of December 2024—more than 2.5 years later—the landlord has not provided written consent to the assignment. RES has been in continuous occupancy and paying rent, and the landlord has accepted rent payments without objection, but formal consent has not been obtained.

The proposed Sycamore acquisition would constitute an additional change-of-control event, potentially requiring a separate consent from the landlord. The facility generates approximately $16.2M in annual revenue (12.7% of total company revenue).

**IMPACT:** Failure to obtain landlord consent could result in lease termination and loss of $16.2M in annual revenue. The unresolved status of the prior assignment creates additional uncertainty regarding the landlord's willingness to consent to the Sycamore transaction.

**MITIGATION:** Obtain landlord consent to the Pinnacle-to-RES assignment immediately. Obtain separate landlord consent to the Sycamore acquisition. Evaluate lease renewal or renegotiation strategy.

---

### RF-004: Environmental Liability at Grand Junction Facility

The Grand Junction headquarters facility (8750 Industrial Parkway, Grand Junction, CO 81505) is owned by RES and has documented soil and groundwater contamination resulting from historical industrial operations conducted by prior owners before RES acquired the property in 2009.

A Phase II Environmental Site Assessment completed in June 2019 identified petroleum hydrocarbon contamination and chlorinated solvent contamination (TCE, PCE) with estimated remediation cost of $1.8M–$2.6M (2019 dollars). No updated Phase II ESA has been commissioned since June 2019.

RES has accrued $3.2M on its balance sheet for environmental remediation obligations across all facilities, with approximately $2.2M attributable to the Grand Junction site.

**IMPACT:** Actual remediation costs may exceed the $3.2M accrual if site conditions have worsened or if off-site migration is identified. Vapor intrusion mitigation costs could be substantial.

**MITIGATION:** Commission updated Phase II ESA prior to closing. Conduct vapor intrusion assessment. Confirm adequacy of $3.2M accrual.

- **RF-005: Pending CDPHE Enforcement Action** – Notice of Violation for improper hazardous waste storage. Penalty exposure: $200K–$1.16M depending on violation period determination.

- **RF-006: Personal Injury Litigation** – Ramirez v. RES personal injury action alleging occupational exposure. Damages sought: $4.8M. Coverage determination pending.

- **RF-009: Change-of-Control Severance Obligations** – Total severance obligations: $3.775M. Single-trigger obligations (Calloway brothers): $2.625M payable within 30 days of closing.

- **RF-011: Aging Fleet and Elevated Capex Requirements** – 44.7% of fleet at or past useful life. Estimated replacement cost: $9.8M.

- **RF-010: EBITDA Reconciliation Discrepancy** – $1.0M unexplained difference between management-presented Adjusted EBITDA ($22.8M) and detailed QofE schedule ($23.8M).

- **RF-019: Insurance Coverage Gaps** – CGL policy contains blanket asbestos exclusion. Pollution liability excludes gradual contamination at owned facilities.

## MEDIUM-SEVERITY RED FLAGS (8)

- **RF-012: Asbestos Long-Tail Liability** – Company operated asbestos abatement service line FY2012–FY2023. Two open workers' compensation claims. Pre-2015 work not covered by current insurance.

- **RF-013: Threatened Wage & Hour Claim** – ~8 former field technicians alleging improper overtime classification. Claimed exposure: $330K.

- **RF-014: Union Organizing Activity** – NLRB election petition filed November 4, 2024 covering 62 field technicians.

- **RF-015: Wyoming Permit Renewal** – Casper facility permit expired November 30, 2024. Renewal application pending.

- **RF-016: Pinnacle Integration Completion** – Pinnacle entity not formally dissolved. Permits and leases in process of transfer.

- **RF-017: Adjusted EBITDA Adjustment Sustainability** – $5.4M in adjustments included in Adjusted EBITDA. Sustainability uncertain.

- **RF-018: Customer Contract Renewals** – Multiple leases expire within 24 months of target closing.

- **RF-020: Revenue Growth Deceleration** – Growth rate declining from 16.8% CAGR to 11.1%.

## CONCLUSION

The proposed acquisition presents a compelling platform opportunity with strong market positioning and experienced management. However, the transaction is subject to material risks that require careful due diligence and mitigation planning.

The four critical red flags must be resolved or adequately mitigated prior to closing. Estimated aggregate contingent liability exposure ranges from $800K to $2.5M and should be factored into purchase price negotiations.

With appropriate due diligence and risk mitigation, this transaction can proceed to successful closing.
